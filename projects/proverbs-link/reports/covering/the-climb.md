# The climb: a man under covering, a man as covering

Readable report: click **Covering** on [`climb.html`](../../climb.html)
→ [`man-under-covering.html`](./man-under-covering.html).

A Scripture-interpreting-Scripture narrative for men. The same door-pattern
holds at every level, for men and for women, though the offices differ. This
piece is aimed at the man who seeks the climb: first, **who he takes refuge
under**; then, **what he is to be** for those who take refuge under him.

---

## 1. The chain that interprets every other covering

> “But I would have you know, that the head of every man is Christ; and the
> head of the woman is the man; and the head of Christ is God.” (1 Corinthians 11:3)

This is not a slogan. It is the grammar of the whole Bible’s covering.

- God is head of Christ.
- Christ is head of every man.
- The man is head of the woman.

No one in the chain is uncovered, including the Son. No one in the chain is
autonomous, including the man who will later be a hiding-place for many. The
climb does not begin with “become a shield.” It begins with “take your place
under your Head.”

The Shield texts name the same order with another word:

> “Every word of God is tried: He is a shield unto them that take refuge in
> him.” (Proverbs 30:5; cf. Psalm 18:30)

> “I am thy shield.” (Genesis 15:1)

A man is not first a cover. He is first a man who comes under the Coverer.

---

## 2. The Son is the pattern of the man

The man who wants to know what covering looks like looks at Christ. The man
who wants to know what coming-under looks like also looks at Christ. The same
Person does both.

> “The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father doing: for
> what things soever he doeth, these the Son also doeth in like manner.”
> (John 5:19)

> “I do nothing of myself, but as the Father taught me, I speak these things.
> … I do always the things that are pleasing to him.” (John 8:28–29)

> “Have this mind in you, which was also in Christ Jesus… he humbled himself,
> becoming obedient even unto death… Wherefore also God highly exalted him.”
> (Philippians 2:5–9)

The Son is not a shield because he broke from the Father. He covers the church
**because** he is under the Father. He then becomes the covering: he gives
himself up for the church (Ephesians 5:25); he says, “if therefore ye seek me,
let these go their way” (John 18:8); he prays not for the Twelve only, but for
those who will believe through their word (John 17:20). The climb in the Son
is obedience first, then a widening cover.

Ephesians 5:1 binds the man to that pattern without remainder: “Be ye
therefore imitators of God, as beloved children.” A son copies the Father’s
covering. He does not invent a new kind of power.

---

## 3. First: take refuge. Do not skip the door.

Psalm 91 does not promise the shadow of the Almighty to the man who merely
admires it.

> “He that **dwelleth** in the secret place of the Most High shall **abide**
> under the shadow of the Almighty. I will say of Jehovah, He is my refuge
> and my fortress; my God, in whom I trust. … He will cover thee with his
> pinions, and under his wings shalt thou take refuge: his truth is a shield
> and a buckler.” (Psalm 91:1–4)

Nahum 1:7: “he knoweth them that take refuge in him.” Psalm 2:12: “Blessed
are all they that take refuge in him.” Matthew 23:37 shows the same covering
refused: “how often would I have gathered thy children… and ye would not.”

The ark, the Passover house, Rahab’s house, and the city of refuge all have
a door. The covering is given. It is enjoyed by those who **enter and remain**
(Genesis 7:1, 16; Exodus 12:22–23; Joshua 2:18–19; Numbers 35:11).

For the man seeking the climb, this is the first office: **be a refugee in
God.** Proverbs 30:5 is his verse before it is anyone else’s. Until he dwells
there, he may hold a title — husband, father, master, prince — but he is
building a house while refusing the Builder.

> “Except Jehovah build the house, they labor in vain that build it: except
> Jehovah keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain.” (Psalm 127:1)

Psalm 127 is a Song of Ascents. The climb of the house is still the Lord’s
keeping.

---

## 4. The centurion’s law: under authority, therefore over men

Jesus marvels at a Gentile soldier because the man understood covering as a
single system:

> “For I also am a man **under** authority, **having under myself** soldiers:
> and I say to this one, Go, and he goeth…” (Matthew 8:9–10)

Scripture interprets Scripture here. The centurion is not claiming that
authority originates in himself. He is a man **under**, therefore a man who
may say **go**. That is 1 Corinthians 11:3 in a Roman helmet. That is John
5:19 in a chain of command. A man who will not be under has no biblical
right to stand over.

This is why the household codes never begin with the man as a bare peak.
They always name the Head above him in the same breath:

- Children obey parents; **fathers** nurture “in the chastening and
  admonition of **the Lord**.” (Ephesians 6:1, 4; Colossians 3:20–21)
- Servants obey earthly masters “**as unto Christ**”; **masters** forbear
  threatening, “knowing that he who is both their Master and yours is in
  heaven.” (Ephesians 6:5, 9; Colossians 4:1)
- Husbands love “**as Christ** also loved the church.” (Ephesians 5:25)
- Every soul is subject to the higher powers, “for there is no power but of
  God.” (Romans 13:1–2)
- The flock obeys rulers who “watch in behalf of your souls, as they that
  shall give account.” (Hebrews 13:17)

At every level a man is **someone’s covering** only as he is **already under
a covering**. The offices differ. The grammar does not.

---

## 5. Men come under assigned heads — including hard ones

The same “come under even if he is not yet a worthy shield” that Peter speaks
to wives in 3:1, Peter has **already** spoken to men in 2:13–18. Scripture
does not invent a male exemption.

> “Be subject to every ordinance of man **for the Lord’s sake**: whether to
> the king, as supreme…” (1 Peter 2:13)

> “Servants, be in subjection to your masters with all fear; not only to the
> good and gentle, **but also to the froward.**” (1 Peter 2:18)

> “For hereunto were ye called: because Christ also suffered for you, leaving
> you an example… when he suffered, threatened not; but committed himself to
> him that judgeth righteously.” (1 Peter 2:21, 23)

The man under a froward master is not declaring that master Isaiah 32:2. He
is taking refuge in the righteous Judge **while remaining under the ordinance
God named**. That is how a man honors God at a level where the human cover is
unbelieving, unjust, or incomplete. Romans 13:2 interprets it: to resist the
power as such is to withstand the ordinance of God.

This is the male form of the door. It is not directed at women in this
narrative, but it is the same pattern: **you enter the covering God assigned;
you do not first audit it into nonexistence.** David living under Saul,
refusing to seize the covering by striking the Lord’s anointed, is the story
form (1 Samuel 24). Joseph under Potiphar and under Pharaoh, Daniel under
pagan kings, the exiles told to seek the peace of Babylon (Jeremiah 29:7) —
men remaining under heads who were not Israel’s shepherds, while still
belonging first to the LORD as shield.

Hebrews 13:17 adds the church level: obey those who rule over you, who will
give account. A man who will not be pastored is already refusing a covering
Christ installed in his body.

Exodus 20:12 is the first human covering a male child is given: honor father
and mother. The climb does not despise the small roof it was born under.

---

## 6. Then: become a hiding-place

Once the man is under God, under Christ, and under the ordinances God has
set, Scripture names what he is to **be**.

> “Behold, a king shall reign in righteousness, and princes shall rule in
> justice. And **a man shall be as a hiding-place** from the wind, and a
> covert from the tempest, as streams of water in a dry place, as the shade
> of a great rock in a weary land.” (Isaiah 32:1–2)

This is the climb’s aim in one sentence. God’s own covering-words — hiding-
place, covert, rock, water — are laid on **a man**, under a righteous king.
Psalm 47:9 and 89:18 interpret the royal form: the princes are “shields,” and
those shields **belong to God**; “our shield belongeth unto Jehovah, and our
king to the Holy One of Israel.” The man-as-shield is real. He is derivative.

Easton’s dictionary notes the same double use of shield: God, and earthly
princes as defenders of their people. The vocation is imitation, not
replacement.

### The widening rings

**The self under God.** Ephesians 6:10–16: be strong **in the Lord**; take up
the shield of faith. The man does not manufacture the shield. He takes it up.
The darts are the evil one’s. His first covering-work is to **stand** where
God has armed him. A man who will not take refuge cannot quench anything for
anyone else.

**The household.** Genesis 18:19 interprets Abraham after Genesis 15:1. God
was already Abram’s shield. Then: “I have known him, to the end that he may
**command his children and his household after him**, that they may keep the
way of Jehovah.” Proverbs 14:26: in the fear of the LORD is strong
confidence, “and his children shall have a place of refuge.” The father’s
refuge in God **becomes** the children’s refuge. Ephesians 6:4 / Colossians
3:21: he covers by nurture, not by provocation. Psalm 127:3: the children
themselves are the Lord’s heritage, not the man’s project.

**The wife.** Ephesians 5:23, 25: the husband is head “as Christ also is head
of the church, being himself the saviour of the body”; he loves “as Christ
also loved the church, and gave himself up for it.” He is not head as a
collector of tribute. He is head as a coverer who dies outward. 1 Peter 3:7:
dwell according to knowledge, giving honor, as joint-heirs, “that your
prayers be not hindered.” His failure as a covert is sin in him. It is not
the Bible’s cue for him to stop being head, nor for him to wait until he
feels worthy before he takes the office. He is to **become** Isaiah 32:2 in
that house.

**Servants / those under his hand.** Ephesians 6:9; Colossians 4:1: the master
has a Master in heaven. The coverer of men is himself a servant. Threatening
is the opposite of a covert.

**The people of God.** 1 Timothy 3:4–5 interprets the next ring by the last
one: if a man does not rule his own house, he cannot take care of the church
of God. The climb is ordered. You do not skip the household to seize a tribe.
Moses already covering Israel still comes under God at the tent of meeting.
David covering the flock, then Judah, then all Israel, still sings, “Jehovah
is my rock… my shield” (2 Samuel 22:3, 31). The larger the cover, the more
publicly he must remain a refugee.

---

## 7. The door still works when he is the covering

The man who has become a covert does not drag the whole earth under his
shade by force. The same locative law applies **downward** that applied
**upward**.

Rahab’s house: whoever remains inside, the coverers will answer for; whoever
goes out, his blood is on his own head (Joshua 2:19). Passover: stay in until
morning (Exodus 12:22). The city of refuge must be fled **to**. Boaz spreads
the garment because Ruth asked to come under it (Ruth 3:9), after she had
already taken refuge under the LORD’s wings (Ruth 2:12).

So the man seeking the climb is not told to police the cosmos. He is told to
**be** a hiding-place, and to keep the door what God made it: those who belong
to that house, that marriage, that flock, that charge, come under it. He does
not make himself God. He does not shrug off those God put in his house because
they are difficult. He also does not pretend he can cover a man who will not
dwell anywhere.

His work is the Father’s work in miniature: offer real cover, keep the door,
stand in the gap (Ezekiel 22:30), give himself up.

---

## 8. False climbs Scripture already judged

Scripture interprets the true climb by exposing the false ones.

- **Korah** will not come under Moses and Aaron; he wants the covering without
  the Head who assigned it (Numbers 16). The earth does not reward that
  ascent.
- **Saul** keeps the name of king while refusing the word of the LORD
  (1 Samuel 15). A title without refuge is not a shield.
- **Solomon’s gold shields** enlarge display; Shishak carries them off;
  Rehoboam hangs bronze (1 Kings 10:16–17; 14:25–28). Scale without
  righteousness is not fatherhood.
- **Psalm 33:16–20**: a king is not saved by a great army; the horse is a vain
  thing; the LORD is “our help and our shield.” Means are not the Coverer.
- **Psalm 118:8**: “It is better to take refuge in Jehovah than to put
  confidence in man.” The man-as-covert never replaces the LORD-as-shield.
  Those under a man still take refuge in God; that is how Sarah hoped in God
  while under Abraham (1 Peter 3:5–6), and how a son honors a father without
  calling him the Most High.

The false climb is always the same: be over without being under; be large
without being a hiding-place; be a shield in name while the people inside are
weather-beaten.

---

## 9. The narrative a man walks

Read in order, the Scriptures we have used tell one story:

1. **God is the shield** of those who take refuge in him. (Genesis 15:1;
   Proverbs 30:5; Psalm 18:30; Psalm 91:1–4)
2. **The Son takes refuge in the Father** and does what he sees the Father
   do; therefore he covers many. (John 5:19; John 17:20; John 18:8;
   Philippians 2:5–9; Ephesians 5:25)
3. **The head of every man is Christ.** (1 Corinthians 11:3)
4. **Because he is under authority, he may have men under him.**
   (Matthew 8:8–10)
5. **He comes under the ordinances God names** — parents, masters, kings,
   pastors — including the froward, for the Lord’s sake, committing himself
   to the righteous Judge. (Exodus 20:12; Romans 13:1–2; 1 Peter 2:13–23;
   Hebrews 13:17)
6. **He takes up the shield of faith** and stands. (Ephesians 6:10–16)
7. **He becomes a hiding-place**: husband who gives himself up; father whose
   fear of the LORD is his children’s refuge; master who remembers his
   Master; and, if God widens the charge, a man like a covert from the
   tempest. (Ephesians 5:23–25; Proverbs 14:26; Ephesians 6:4, 9;
   1 Timothy 3:4–5; Isaiah 32:1–2; Psalm 47:9)
8. **Those given to him come under that covering**, as under every covering
   God made: by remaining in the house. (Joshua 2:19; Ruth 2:12–3:9)
9. **He never stops being a refugee.** The larger the roof, the more he
   sings, “Jehovah is my shield.” (2 Samuel 22:31; Psalm 127:1)

That is the climb. Not the ascent of an uncovered man into godhood. The
ascent of a son who stays under the Father, and therefore can spread a
larger and larger garment — as the Father does — over more of his people.

---

## 10. How the pattern falls on men and women without changing

The offices are not interchangeable. 1 Corinthians 11:3, Ephesians 5, and
1 Peter 3 still name man as head of woman. This narrative has not been a
charge to wives; it has been the man’s path.

The **grammar** is shared:

- Every soul takes refuge under a covering God assigned.
- Covering is enjoyed by remaining under it.
- Coming under is for the Lord’s sake, not because the human cover has
  already become a perfect hiding-place.
- The one who covers must himself be under God, and must actually become
  shade, water, and rock to those inside — or he answers to the Head for
  a false shield.

A man seeking the climb therefore measures himself twice every day, in the
same words Scripture has already joined:

**Under whom do I take refuge?**  
**For whom am I a covert?**

If the first answer is not the LORD, the second answer is vanity. If the
first answer is the LORD, the second answer is his life’s work.
