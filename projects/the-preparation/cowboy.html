Cowboy

Cycle 3 · 9th-grade Spark — ride, rope, shoe, and work livestock on real land. The climax is a ranch apprenticeship week with a signed skills checklist — not a dude-ranch vacation.

9th-grade Spark Light TJC OK Land · after Hunter Draft — pick & choose

Why this cycle — earn your place on the land

Summary: Hunter taught him to take from the land with ethics. Cowboy teaches him to work the land — animals that depend on you, gear that fails if you skip maintenance, weather you cannot negotiate. He rides, ropes, handles livestock, and finishes a real ranch block where a foreman would notice if he slacked. GC and history give context; saddle hours are the adventure.

  • Ride before romance. Lessons or a structured camp first — then open range with a mentor who will correct bad habits.
  • Rope on a dummy, then live. Ground work before heroics. A missed loop is cheaper than a kicked horse.
  • Ranch week is the climax. Working cattle ranch internship or a booked ranch-skills week — chores, feeding, fencing, moving stock. Mentor letter + signed checklist + photo log.
  • Overlaps Hunter ethics. Same conservation thread: know what you manage, don't waste, give back (habitat, stock care, AgriLife projects).
  • Not this year: Uruguay maxim trip, farrier certification, rodeo competition as the bar, or substituting a trail ride for ranch work.

The call: show up before dawn, do the unglamorous chore, ask the ranch lead what broke and how they fixed it. A man who can work stock is harder to fake than a man who can post on Instagram.

Book the ranch week before spring. East TX working-ranch opportunities fill early. Idaho-style horseback camp is a later optional upgrade once basics exist — not a substitute for the TX climax.

  • Rodeo competition as the only proof
  • Uruguay maxim ranch (save for later)

Wrong pace

  • Open-range gallop before balance
  • Live roping without dummy reps
  • Stock work without first aid

Not required

  • Bio CLEP before class spine
