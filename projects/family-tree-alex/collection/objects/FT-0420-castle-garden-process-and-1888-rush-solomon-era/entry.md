## What Castle Garden processing looked like (NPS)

NPS *Castle Garden Emigrant Depot* page (deeper than the History & Culture index cited on FT-0415):

- America’s first facility dedicated to immigrant welfare: **3 Aug 1855 – 30 Apr 1890**
- Through **31 Dec 1889**: **8,280,917** of **10,956,910** U.S. immigrants (**~75%**) passed through
- Officials recorded names, vessel, destination, money on hand, relatives already in the U.S.
- **Registry desk:** two lines (English vs other languages); translators; the rotunda was famously loud — Yiddish speakers coined **"Kesselgarden"** for noisy chaos
- **Baggage:** stalls after 1857 reforms; later capacity up to ~**15,000** pieces; storage up to **six days**
- **Exchange Office** posted live Wall Street rates; **Railroad Office** sold NY & Erie / NY Central / Pennsylvania Central tickets
- **Bathing rooms** before exit; **Labor Exchange** (built **1867**); hospital and information bureau in the outer yard

Source: [NPS — Castle Garden Emigrant Depot](https://www.nps.gov/cacl/learn/historyculture/castle-garden-emigrant-depot.htm)

## Same-year newspaper color (1888)

### The Sun, 8 Apr 1888 — “The Rush of Immigration”

Immigration had spring/fall seasons “just as there are semi-annual seasons for selling dry goods, shoes, and jewelry.” Immigrants were arriving at about **1,000 a day**; **53,817** in the three months ending 31 Mar (up ~3,000 vs 1887). Supt. **Henry J. Jackson** expected **≥100,000** through the Garden in the next three months.

On Russian Jews (OCR cleaned lightly):

> You notice that the immigration of Russian Jews is large. They, however, seldom leave the big cities, as most of them are **tailors**, and their little shops dot the side streets of cities from New York to San Francisco.

Source: [The Sun, 8 Apr 1888, p.13](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83030272/1888-04-08/ed-1/?sp=13)

### Evening World, 18 Aug 1888 — “Castle Garden Methods”

Jackson before the Ford committee (weeks after Solomon’s July window): busy days ~**4,000** arrivals (peak day ~**9,000**); immigrants made to pass a **narrow aisle in single file** and answer about means, criminal record, assisted passage; **360** returned immediately the prior year. On Eastern European Jews:

> Most of the Russians, Hungarians and Polish Jews land with all their effects in their hands, but they seldom become paupers…

Baggage contractors handled on the order of **100,000+** pieces a year; money-changers worked Wall Street rates; rail agents sold through tickets (Chicago sample **$5** after monopoly changes).

Source: [Evening World, 18 Aug 1888, p.4](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83030193/1888-08-18/ed-1/?sp=4)
