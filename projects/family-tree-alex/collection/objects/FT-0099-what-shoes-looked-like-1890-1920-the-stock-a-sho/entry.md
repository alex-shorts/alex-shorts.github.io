## Catalog note

**Material culture for the shoe-jobber story.** Monroe’s cert says “Jobber, shoe business” ([FT-0094](../FT-0094/entry.md)). These images show what **factory-made footwear looked like** and how **retailers displayed it** — the goods a wholesaler moved between factory and store.

## Glossary (period styles)

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **High lace / button boots** | Ankle-to-calf boots — everyday street wear for women into the 1910s; buttons or laces up the front |
| **Oxfords** | Low shoes with closed lacing over the instep — men’s standard; women’s “cooler” day shoe by ~1900 |
| **Colonial / Cuban-heel low shoe** | Fashionable low shoe with a buckle and blocky heel (Crawford ad 1902) |
| **Kid / patent calf** | Soft goatskin vs glossy patent leather finishes named in ads |
| **Jobber’s role** | Bought these **by the case** from makers; sold assortments to stores like the interiors below |

Crawford Shoe ad (*Life*, 1902):

> Oxfords will be worn more extensively than ever before by both men and women. These cool, comfortable, fashionable shoes are shown in **61 styles** in the Crawford… best ideas of New York, London and Paris custom boot makers.
