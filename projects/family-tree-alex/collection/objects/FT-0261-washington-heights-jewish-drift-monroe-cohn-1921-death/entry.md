## Catalog note

**Last-address story.** Monroe Cohn died **25 Dec 1921** at **245 Fort Washington Avenue** ([FT-0023](../FT-0023-nyc-death-certificate-monroe-cohn-manhattan-1921/entry.md)) — in **Washington Heights**, the one Manhattan section whose Jewish population **grew** while the Lower East Side emptied in the same decade.

## The drift (survey language)

From the **Jewish Communal Survey** (reported JTA, 14 Mar 1928):

> Manhattan was divided into eight borough sections … Lower East Side, Central East Side, Lower West Side, West End, Yorkville, Harlem, West Harlem and **Washington Heights**. Of these eight sections, **Washington Heights alone showed an increase** in the number of Jews, from **24,511 in 1915 to 41,320 in 1925**, or a gain of 68.6 percent.

> … **Washington Heights 23.9 percent Jewish** … may be expected to attract Jewish populations for some time to come.

The survey describes the **onward march from the tenement districts of the lower East Side** to Yorkville, Harlem, Bronx, and middle-class areas — the same northward movement that carries this family from **East 60th Street at marriage (1893)** to **Fort Washington at death (1921)**.
