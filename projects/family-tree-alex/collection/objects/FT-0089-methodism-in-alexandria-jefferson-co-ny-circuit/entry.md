## Catalog note

**Dorland generation place story:** while Dorland’s own charge list is still thin, his son Earl was born in **Alexandria, Jefferson County** — the same Thousand Islands county as Grindstone. Local reminiscence shows how Methodism arrived there by **circuit riders on horseback**, then settled into chapels, long Sunday services, class meetings, and outdoor quarterly conferences.

## Full text (story excerpts)

### Circuit riders open the town

> In 1827 the Methodists organized a society which has always been permanent. It was in the school house and the homes that the services of these Methodists were conducted by **circuit riders who went from place to place on horseback**. The Alexandria Circuit was formed in 1831 and comprised all preaching places in the Town of Alexandria and Theresa…

### A child’s Sunday (William Avery)

> …I can remember being packed into the family buggy on Sunday morning at nine o’clock… Arriving at Plessis church at ten o’clock… At 11 the church service started… the minister spoke for **one hour**. … By 12:30 or shortly after the service closed, and then the old-fashioned **class meeting** convened which lasted for another hour. … By 2:30 our family back home and ready for dinner. It was not unusual for father and mother to drive to Barnes Settlement or Alexandria Bay to attend **another service** later in the day.

### Outdoor quarterly meetings

> Quarterly conferences were held four times a year with the presiding elder in attendance. … In the summer these meetings were held outdoors in the Overacker woods in Barnes Settlement…

### “Daddy Ayres” and the snowbank (kid humor)

> One very stormy day… [Rev. Ayres] asked father to go with him to Alexandria Bay. Father said… he didn’t think they could get thru the snow. Daddy Ayres responded, “I know no such word as ‘can’t.’” … About a mile down the road they tipped over in a snow bank. … When they returned mother couldn’t resist asking if he now knew the meaning of “can’t.”
