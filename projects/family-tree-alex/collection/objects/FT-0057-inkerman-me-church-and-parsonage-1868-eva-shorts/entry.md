## Catalog note

Documents the **Inkerman Methodist Episcopal** white-frame church (**1868**, Matilda Circuit) that **boasted a parsonage** — the building context for Eva Eliza Shorts’s death at the **M.E. Parsonage, Inkerman, 9 Aug 1876** — and the **1883 red-brick** church built when Wesleyan + ME congregations combined (still standing as Inkerman United).

## Full text

### Mills — Methodism chapter (Inkerman ME building & parsonage)

In the early days of Inkerman the population belonged to the Methodist denomination, specifically divided between the **Wesleyan Methodist** and the **Methodist Episcopalians**. These two groups later combined to build a single Methodist Church in **1883**. Methodist Episcopalians came to Canada from the United States; Wesleyan Methodists from Britain.

**Inkerman Methodist Episcopal (M.E.) Church**

- Built in **1868** as part of the **Matilda Circuit**.
- A **white frame building** that **boasted a parsonage**.
- Situated **towards the west of the village**.
- Dedication service **14 February 1871**, conducted by Revs. S.G. Stone, B. Lane, and J.F. Wilson.
- A collection and tea-meeting raised $103; by **1875** the church and parsonage were completely paid for.
- Details cited from *The Canadian Christian Advocate* (ME church newspaper).

**Inkerman Wesleyan Methodist Church**

- Built in **1857** as part of the **Kemptville Circuit**.
- Northeast corner of the village with burying grounds beside it.
- Frame building, 30 × 40 feet, with arrangements for side and end galleries.
- Cited from the *Christian Guardian*.

**Union / new brick church**

Around **1880**, economic difficulty maintaining two buildings led the congregations to combine. Planning for a new **red brick church** began late **1879**; cornerstone **15 August 1883**; building completed **1883**. Pastor at dedication era: Rev. John / J. Stewart (bell inscription Dec. 25, 1883).

### fadedgenes — Inkerman Methodist Church (gazetteer + 1983 clipping)

Village gazetteers place Methodist (and Anglican) churches at Inkerman. 1983 local clipping: Wesleyan Methodists and Methodist Episcopalians combined to erect the new church; red brick building “still stands proudly at the entrance to the village.” Published minister list on that page (1859–1925) does **not** name Alexander Shorts — list appears post-union / mixed; Eva’s 1876 ME-parsonage death is independent (KNS 96 / person.md).

### Knight’s Cemetery header (burial adjacency)

Open transcript header: “The Methodist Episcopal Church was founded in Inkerman in **1855** and the Wesleyan Methodist in **1882**” (dates differ slightly from Mills’s building chronology — both open sources retained). Eva stone **KNS 96**: “In Memory of Eva Eliza wife of Rev. Alex. Shorts…”
