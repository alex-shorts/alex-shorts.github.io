## Catalog note

Compiled STCC biography of **Col. Hugh Maxwell** (1733–1799): Minute-Men → Prescott / Bunker Hill wound; Continental promotions (major **7 Jul 1777**; Lt. Col. 3rd Mass. under Maj. Gen. William Heath); **Society of the Cincinnati**; eloquent **1783** appeal to William Stickney on unpaid soldiers; opposed Shays’ Rebellion as Heath selectman / JP; unsuccessful **1795** pension petition; West Indies horse cargo; d. at sea **14 Oct 1799**.

## Full text

> In April 1775, Lieutenant Hugh Maxwell marched to Cambridge with his Charlemont militia company. Maxwell remained at Cambridge, where he joined Colonel William Prescott's regiment. In June 1775, Hugh fought and was wounded at the battle of Bunker Hill.

> In November, 1776, Captain Hugh Maxwell was ordered to raise a company of 86 "able-bodied and effective Men" to serve in the Continental Army "'til the End of the present War or for the Term of three Years." He was commissioned as a major on July 7, 1777. In addition to the siege of Boston, he was present at the battles of Trenton, Princeton, Saratoga, and Monmouth. By the end of the war, he was Lieutenant Colonel of the Third Massachusetts Regiment of Major General William Heath's brigade.

> Deeply concerned for his men, Colonel Maxwell wrote an eloquent appeal to William Stickney of Massachusetts on their behalf… "will you turn us into the wide world to seek our fortune like fortune-hunters, without money or friends, after we have served you eight years in your wars? after spending the flower of our days in the service of our country…"

> After his discharge, Colonel Maxwell… immediately joined the Society of the Cincinnati… Maxwell expressed his admiration for his commanding officer General Heath when the section of Charlemont in which Maxwell lived was incorporated as the new town of Heath.

> …Colonel Maxwell opposed the movement during the winter of 1786-87 that later became known as Shays' Rebellion. In contrast to nearby Colrain, not a single Heath man took up arms against the government. That spring, Selectmen Hugh Maxwell and Asaph White submitted a reimbursement request… for… 11 men who had traveled to defend the United States Arsenal at Springfield. As a justice of the peace, Colonel Maxwell administered the Oath of Allegiance to former insurgents.

> Hugh traveled to Philadelphia in 1795, where he petitioned the United States Congress unsuccessfully for a pension… In 1799… shipping horses to the West Indies… died at sea on October 14, 1799, at the age of 67.
