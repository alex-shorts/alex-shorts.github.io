## Catalog note

**Secondary / local-historian.** Mills compiled history states that in the **1840s–1850s** the trail through **Coonville** carried the **Western Stage Line** from **Kanesville (Council Bluffs)** via the **Plumer Inn** (east of Silver City) and the **Hoyt House** **enroute to St. Joseph, Missouri**. Soft landscape support for the FT-0177 meet/freight beat — **not** proof of John W’s ownership of a named freight firm.

## Full text

**Early Transportation — Stagecoach** (*Mills County General History*, IAGenWeb):

> The Indian trails gave way to paths for the Mormon travellers and land seekers. During the 1840's and 1850's, the trail through **Coonville** supported the **Western Stage Line** from **Kanesville, now Council Bluffs**, to the **Plumer Inn** east of Silver City, on to the **Hoyt House** enroute to **St. Joseph, Missouri** and points south. Later this would be a route that the railroads took to cross the plains into other territories.

**Coonville rename (same page, Organization):**

> When the Mormons moved westward, Rushville became known as **Coonville**, in honor of the first physician of the community, Dr. Liberius T. Coon, who settled there in 1848… The name of the county seat was changed in **1853** from Coonville to **Glenwood**.
