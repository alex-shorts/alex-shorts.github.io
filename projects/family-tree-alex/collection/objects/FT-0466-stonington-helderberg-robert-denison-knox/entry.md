## Catalog note

**How the Stonington Denisons leave the Cromwell/Mystic country.** After the Revolution, Stephen Van Rensselaer III advertised **“free”** Helderberg lots for veterans. Stonington families scouted and moved **~1787–1790**. Miller’s list includes **“Children of Daniel DENISON and Esther WHEELER: Robert and Hannah.”** That is our **Robert** (b. 1749), father of **Nathan** (b. Mystic 1781). Wheeler independently: Robert × Anna Chesebrough, **“About 1793 he went to Knox, New York.”** Nathan then married **Elizabeth Thompson at Berne, 15 Dec 1803**, and the couple settled **Napanee / Richmond** (already FT-0105). The “free” land was a **lease**: wheat, fat fowls, a day’s team, and a quarter-sale to the patroon. Many later gave up and moved on — Nathan’s Canada move sits in that world without proving he fled rent.

## Excerpts

### Miller / Helderberg Hilltowns

> About 1787 fliers were distributed in Stonington, and elsewhere in New England, advertising “free” land in Western Albany County, New York for veterans of the Revolutionary War. Many Stonington families accepted the offer and moved to Berne and Knox in the Helderberg hills, some to their later regret.

> In order to populate the unsettled lands of the Manor, handbills were distributed throughout New England announcing that he would give veterans of the Revolution homesteads without cost. Only after the farms became productive would he ask for any compensation. A group of Stonington families sent some members to look over the land in Albany County. Between about 1787 and 1790 the following families from Stonington and elsewhere in New London County moved to western Albany County:
>
> … **Children of Daniel DENISON and Esther WHEELER: Robert and Hannah** …

> After seven years of clearing the land and building their homes they were required to sign a lease that typically required an annual payment of “…nineteen bushels of good merchantable Winter Wheat, four fat fowls, … and perform one days service with carriage and horse.” Taken from the Indenture made July 2, 1799 between Stephen Van Rensselaer and Nathaniel Gallup and Samuel Gallup.

> “Cash would be accepted in lieu of wheat but very few in the township of Berne [which at that time included Knox] had hard money. … Included in his deed was the onerous quarter sale provision… These conditions were perfectly legal for the deeds were drawn up by none other than Alexander Hamilton…”

> Many families just gave up and moved on west, including many of the families who had moved to Albany County from Stonington.
