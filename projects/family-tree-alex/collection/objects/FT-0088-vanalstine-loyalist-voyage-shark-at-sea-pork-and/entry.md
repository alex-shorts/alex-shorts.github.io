## Full text (excerpt)

> They sailed from New York on the 8th Sept. 1783, and arrived at Quebec, 8th Oct. … Thus, it is told, that after leaving New York a few days, **shark was observed following the vessel**, which created no little consternation. It continued to follow for many days, until a child had died and been consigned to the deep, after which it was no longer seen. The Government rations with which they were supplied, consisted, as the story has been told the writer, of “**pork and peas for breakfast; peas and pork for dinner; and for supper, one or the other.**” The party proceeded from Quebec … thence to **Sorel**, where they spent the winter. They inhabited their **linen tents**, which afforded but little protection from the intense cold.

Same tradition: batteaux along the shore; settlers wishing for particular wooded spots that later fell to them by lot; first burials without roads or ministers (see Casper Hover in FT-0087).
