## Catalog note

**Family-book Cromwell chapter.** Baldwin states George went to England after Bridget’s 1643 death, **served under Cromwell in the army of the Parliament**, was **wounded at Naseby**, was **nursed by Ann** at John Borodell’s house, married her, and returned — later called in Roxbury **“a young soldier lately come out of the wars in England.”** This is the vignette later retellings copy. **Do not treat Naseby as proven:** brother Daniel’s 1672 letter (FT-0406) names **Marston Moor / York** and a prisoner episode, not Naseby.

## Excerpts

### Stem notice (p. 6)

> The wife, Bridget, died in 1643. George Denison then went to England, served under Cromwell in the army of the Parliament, won distinction, was wounded at Naseby, was nursed at the house of John Borodell, by his daughter Ann, was married to Ann, returned to Roxbury, and finally settled at Stonington, Conn.

Children of George × Ann include **#7 John**, b. **14 Jul 1646**, m. Phebe Lay.

### Appendix — return as “young soldier”

> After three years of wedded life, Bridget died, leaving two daughters, Sarah and Hannah… Very soon after her death, he returned to England, enlisted under Cromwell in the army of the Parliament, won distinction, was wounded at Naseby, was nursed at the house of John Borodell by his daughter Ann, which led to his marriage with her, and his early return to Roxbury, where he was chosen Captain, and was called, “a young soldier lately come out of the wars in England.” He is said to have had one son, John, born July 14, 1646, when he came to Roxbury the second time, which would make his absence about three years.

### Appendix — he did not already know Ann

Baldwin prints George’s 1640 verse letter to Bridget in order to **reject** a descendant romance that he had been betrothed to Ann before emigrating as a boy:

> There is no evidence of this, but much to the contrary. He was but thirteen when he emigrated, and probably never heard of Ann Borodell until he was carried a wounded soldier to her father’s house, John Borodell of Cork, Ireland, (who was then living in England,) where Ann became his nurse, and afterward his wife.

**Cork / Irish-lord color here is tradition.** FT-0410 (Miscia/Kasper): Ann’s father was **Cumbrian**, not an Irish lord or a Cromwell soldier; marriage in **England** after Bridget’s death still holds.

## Supports

- Supports: Compiled claim that George served **Parliament / Cromwell** after 1643; Naseby-wound + Ann-nurse **tradition**; Roxbury nickname **young soldier out of the wars in England**; son **John** 1646 after the return; first marriage to Bridget, not a prior Ann betrothal
- Does not support: Named regiment, rank on a muster, or unit under Cromwell; that Naseby is the proven battle (conflicts with FT-0406 Marston Moor); John Borodell of Cork / Irish lord; hard vitals overwrite
