## Regimental narrative (Shiloh arrival)

*Genealogy Trails*, 18th Wisconsin Infantry sketch:

> This regiment was organized in Oct., 1861. It was mustered in and left the state **March 30, 1862** for **Pittsburg Landing** and reached there **April 5**. The next morning, with **absolutely no instruction in the manual of arms and but little drill**, it was ordered to check the enemy's advance at **Shiloh**. It fought bravely, losing **24 killed, 82 wounded and 174 prisoners**. **"Many regiments may well covet the impressions which the 18th Wis. left of personal bravery heroic daring and determined endurance,"** said Gov. Harvey.

Regimental loss table (Shiloh, 6 Apr 1862): **23 killed · 35 died of wounds · 186 captured/missing · 246 total** (same source).

Field command losses that day included **Col. James S. Alban** (died of wounds **7 Apr 1862**) — the green regiment's first battle was its bloodiest.

## What a surgeon met (honest framing)

The sketch does **not** quote Buck by name at Shiloh. It establishes:

- The **18th Wisconsin** entered its **first combat** essentially **untrained** on the **second day** of Shiloh.
- **Casualties were immediate and heavy** — the surgical workload for a newly appointed assistant surgeon would have been mass trauma care, not garrison clinic work.
- Personal promotion to **Surgeon** "soon after" Shiloh is on **FT-0360** / **FT-0200**, not this regimental page.
