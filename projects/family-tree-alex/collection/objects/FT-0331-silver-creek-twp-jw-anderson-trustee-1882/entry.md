## Catalog note

**Secondary / local-historian.** Pottawattamie **Silver Creek Township** sketch (1882/1907 histories) places **J. W. Anderson** among **1882 township trustees** and lists **John Anderson** among early settlers — civic/farm presence on the same creek corridor named in the Silver City Community History homestead (FT-0177). Companion stage-station narrative documents the Wheeler’s Grove–Council Bluffs stage route through the township (landscape for freight-era travel; does not name John W.).

## Full text

**Township officers / settlers** (Pottawattamie County Genealogical Society digest of 1882 & 1907 histories):

> Township officers in 1882 were: **J. W. Anderson**, James Summers and Mr. Brauchman, trustees; S. H. Gregory, clerk, and Isaac Hoopes, assessor.
>
> Settlers: Pleasant Taylor, a native of Tenn; Mr. Gardner; Issac Moore; John Bratten; The McHanna brothers; Ole Lawson & Henry Ouren (1866); Malcolm McKenzie (1867); **John Anderson**
>
> Stage line: Mr. Gardner. It was between Wheeler’s Grove and Council Bluffs. He sold to Issac Moore and in 1854, Moore sold to John Bratten.

**Stage station color** (IAGenWeb Silver Creek Township history — same county-history tradition):

> The first man to open a stage station between Wheeler's Grove and Council Bluffs was a Mr. Gardner, and this was the only one between the two points. … During the Pike's Peak excitement, the station was a lively point; from 60 to 70 teams would pass through daily. In 1856, five hundred Mormon emigrants passed through on the stage road with hand carts, not a single horse in the entire outfit.
