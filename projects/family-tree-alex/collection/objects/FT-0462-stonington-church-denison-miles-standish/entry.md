## Catalog note

**How the English veteran was remembered in his own town.** The 1874 Stonington church book calls him the **Miles Standish of the settlement** and narrates the **Canonchet** capture and later Talcott marches. This is the New England half of the same soldier who (by family story) had already fought for Parliament in England. Indigenous war history here is **violent and contested** — quote the 1874 text; do not celebrate it.

## Excerpts

> While Captain Denison was prominent and active in civil affairs, he was more distinguished in military matters. With the exception of Captain John Mason, he was the most conspicuous and daring soldier of New London County, and was in fact the Miles Standish of the settlement, a natural military leader, and though holding the rank of captain, he often commanded expeditions against the Indians, and was always most successful when commander-in-chief.

> He participated in the Narraganset swamp fight in 1675, and performed prodigies of valor. As early as February following a series of forays were commenced against the Narraganset Indians. They were commanded by Captain Denison and Capt. James Avery. These partisan bands were composed of volunteers, regular soldiers, Pequots, Mohegans and Niantics.

> It was the third of these roving excursions, begun in March and ended April 10, 1676, in which the celebrated Narraganset chieftain, Canonchet, was taken prisoner. He was brought to Stonington, and was put to death at Anguilla…

The 1874 sketch then gives Canonchet’s reported answers to the council of war (“he was now in their hands…”) and the execution by allied sachems. **Details of the killing vary by secondary;** this Accession preserves one local compiled version, not a unique primary.

> In June following, Captain Denison commanded a company raised in New London County, for Major Talcott’s expedition against the Indians in Massachusetts. They went as far north as Northampton… This time they went first to the northwest of Providence, then south to Point Judith, then home through Westerly and Stonington to New London… July 18, 1676… into Plymouth Colony… a sharp and final struggle… beyond the Housatonic.

> Captain Denison… died at Hartford, October 24, 1694, during the session of the General Court, which he was attending officially, and was buried there.

## Supports

- Supports: 19th-c. Stonington church memory of George as the town’s chief soldier after Mason; swamp fight; Avery forays; Canonchet taken to Stonington / Anguilla; Talcott 1676 marches; death at Hartford while at court
- Does not support: Cromwell / Naseby (not in this sketch); a kinder reading of the war; exact Canonchet quotes as courtroom transcript
