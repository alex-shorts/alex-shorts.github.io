## Full text

### Bibliographic

**Yesterday And To-Day: The History Of Inkerman United Church, 1883–1983**  
Shirley Mills · Inkerman, Ontario · July 1983  
Canadiana / Seaway Valley Libraries copy (call 287.92)

Cover photo: red-brick Inkerman United Church with corner tower/steeple.

Historic photo caption: **“INKERMAN CHURCH SHOWING CARRIAGE SHEDS”** (pre-spire-removal era).

### Methodism — two churches, then 1883 union (book pp. 7–9 cluster)

There were a number of religions in Canada in those early days, but in Inkerman the people belonged to the Methodist denomination. There were two churches, the **Wesleyan Methodist** and the **Methodist Episcopalians**. … the villagers combined to build a Methodist Church in **1883**…

Originally Methodism came to Canada from two directions. The Methodist Episcopalians were from the States and the Wesleyan Methodists from Britain. …

Inkerman was part of the **Matilda Circuit** until **1867**. …

### ME church + parsonage (book p. 9 — critical for Eva 1876)

The Methodist Episcopal Church became the head of its own circuit in **1867**.  
Building built **1868** (Matilda Circuit context): a **white frame building** that **boasted a parsonage**, located towards the **west of the village**.  
Dedicated **February 14, 1871** (services by Rev. S.G. Stone, B. Lane, and J.F. Wilson).  
The church and parsonage were **completely paid for by 1875**.  
A building was destroyed by fire in **January 1882** and rebuilt with brick by **December 1882**.

Wesleyan Methodist building noted **1857** (Kemptville Circuit), northeast corner of the village with **burying grounds beside it**.

By 1880, maintaining two buildings was difficult; planning to combine began late **1879**; **red brick church** built **1883**.

### The Church To-Day (book p. 1)

At the entrance to the village, on the southside of the roadway, stands the large red brick United Church… It was built in **1883 [a Methodist Church then]**… The spire above the bell tower was removed… after being struck by lightning… otherwise the church is much as it was in 1883.

### Village / FAN color (selected)

- Village named **Inkerman** 1855 (Crimean War battle, 5 Nov 1854); post office 1 Jan 1855 (John Renwick first postmaster).
- 1880s pop. ~400; **two Methodist churches**.
- Continuous century families named in 1983: Bailey, Barkley, Baldwin, Guy, Levere, McMenomy, Suffel, Timmins.

### Later parsonage land note (book p. 11)

A third section of land for a **parsonage** purchased **July 1900** from William and Ida May Higginson — this is the **post-1883 United/Methodist** parsonage site, **not** the 1868–1875 ME parsonage where Eva died.
