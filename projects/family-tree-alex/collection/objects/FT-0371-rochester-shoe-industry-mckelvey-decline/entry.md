## McKelvey beats (verbatim sense)

From *Rochester History* XV:2 (1953), open PDF:

- For half a century Rochester ranked among the country’s leading shoe towns; for years the chief **style center for women’s footwear**.
- Two protracted stoppages; the second and most spectacular in **1922**, “shortly before its decline set in.”
- After 1922: “The great days of the Rochester shoe industry were over”; style-show coverage and national Reporter columns collapsed; several firms liquidated.
- Late-1920s revival on a **cheaper** product briefly boosted census rank to fifth among U.S. shoe towns, but “proved unsound… incapable of surviving the great depression. Shoe plants closed their doors shortly after the storm broke, and many of them failed to reopen.”
- Survivors clung to specialty / quality lines (Armstrong “Balance-in-Motion,” Jumping Jack, branded women’s lines via E. P. Reed).
