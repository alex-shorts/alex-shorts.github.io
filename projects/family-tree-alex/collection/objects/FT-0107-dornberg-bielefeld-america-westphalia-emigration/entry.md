## Catalog note

**History lesson:** Mid-19th-century German (Westphalian) emigration — linen-region pressure, chain migration to Midwest farmland, peak **1852/54** just before Bielefeld industrialization opened local factory futures.  
**Family hinge:** Confirmed **Frederick William August Tuxhorn**, born **1829 Dornberg** (Bielefeld hinterland), died **1884 McPherson County, Kansas** — same sending district Minninger studies; later Gloria Shorts’s maternal grandfather’s father.

## Minninger synthesis (open PDF)

English intro (Stadtarchiv): emigration research starts after Napoleonic Westphalia; Bielefeld became the region’s rising industrial magnet; **1852/54** climax as impoverished weavers, spinners, and rural underclass left for America — especially **Missouri and Illinois** via chain migration — while cheap farmland and wages drew them. **Dornberg district emigration files are entirely missing**; Old Town Bielefeld church books largely lost in WWII — researchers lean on residence lists, Müller legal/clandestine series, and U.S. follow-ups.

German front matter same thesis: peak before industrialization opened futures for linen workers; Missouri/Illinois chain migration.
