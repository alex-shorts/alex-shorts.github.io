## Catalog note

**Museum interpretive label** (PVMA / American Centuries): Col. Hugh Maxwell joined Massachusetts Society for Promoting Agriculture **1794**; veteran of French and Indian War; settled near Charlemont; Revolutionary activist / Provincial Congress; **1785** led village separation forming Heath. Certificate dated **4 Jan 1794**, Heath.

## Full text

> Advances in American agriculture lagged in the years after the Revolutionary War… The Massachusetts Society for Promoting Agriculture was founded in 1792, and in 1794, Colonel Hugh Maxwell joined. He was a veteran of the French and Indian War and settled near Charlemont. During the Revolution, he was an activist and served in the Provincial Congress. In 1785, Maxwell was instrumental in leading his village to separate from Charlement to form the new town of Heath, named after his former commander in the French and Indian War.

## Notes

- **Label error (flag):** Town named for Gen. **William Heath** of the **Revolutionary** Continental Army (White / Recorder / *Christian Patriot* / Everts). Calling him Maxwell’s “former commander in the **French and Indian War**” is unsupported by those narratives — do not import that clause as fact.
- Civic/agriculture post-war color, not a battle story; kept as local museum prose naming Col. Hugh.
