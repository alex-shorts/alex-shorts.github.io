## Arcadia Fire Department (municipal history)

City of Arcadia “About Us” history: department organized **1909** under dual police/fire chief Charles S. Smith; one paid fireman (J.M. Nellis) plus ~30 volunteers; earlier only a hose reel at old City Hall. By **1938**, Nellis as Fire Chief with **19 full-time** firefighters — end of the volunteer system. Later growth (1950s–70s): fire-prevention inspector/Fire Marshal work, new HQ and Station 2 (**1958**), first truck/snorkel company (**1963**), paramedic ambulance (**1973**). Line-of-duty death of Captain Jerry Lee Broadwell (**22 Aug 1975**) memorialized in the library children’s reading room.

Honest limit: No open Arcadia roster naming Richard; years of Arcadia service unknown.

## Huntington Park (municipal → county)

Local fire-service histories: Huntington Park ran its own department until the **1950s**, when the city contracted **Los Angeles County Fire**; former municipal stations became **LACoFD 164** and **165**. Later community lore calls Station 164 “The Big House” (modern reporting — after Richard’s era). Whether Richard served under the **municipal** HPFD or early **county** contract depends on hire dates still unknown.

## Burbank — local historian narratives

**George W. Myers, *The Burbank Community Book* (1944)** — “The Big Fire” (2–3 Dec **1927**): vineyard burn near La Crescenta jumped the Verdugo Hills; **110 Burbank homes** destroyed in hours (~$500,000 local loss); Chief **Homer Davis**’s department laid **2,200 ft** of new hose; mutual aid from Glendale, Los Angeles, North Hollywood and others (**eleven** departments at peak); civilians pressed (Elks Hall tuxedos among hose crews); women brought coffee and sandwiches. Department lore — **before** Richard’s career, but the city’s remembered fire culture.

**City of Burbank / *A History of Burbank* (1967)** (wesclark.com local-history mirror): first city fire truck by **1916**; volunteer force replaced by a regular department with valley branch stations; postwar boom — by **1950** population **78,224**, fire department “added new stations”; photo of personnel and apparatus, Feb **1950**, at the new city hall; by **1961** a new main fire station on Olive Ave plus two more stations. Fits the era when a postwar hire could rise to **Captain**.
