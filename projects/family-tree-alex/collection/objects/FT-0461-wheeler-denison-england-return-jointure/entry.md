## Catalog note

**Stonington town historian** tells the England chapter from **Daniel’s letter**, then proves the Ann marriage by the **£300 jointure** George later recorded at Hartford. Useful because the same book that calls George Connecticut’s greatest soldier after Mason **does not copy Baldwin’s Naseby wound**.

## Excerpts

### Daniel’s letter (Wheeler quoting NEHGR Apr 1892)

> This Capt. George Denison having buried his wife in the year 1643, went back to England the same year, where, as we learn from a letter of his brother, Maj. Gen. Daniel Denison… he says: “My brother George was a soldier there above a year, was at the battle of York, or Marston Moor, where he did good service, and was afterwards taken prisoner, but got free and married a second wife, Miss Ann Borodell, and with her returned to New England in the year 1645…”

Wheeler adds (local estimate, not the 1672 letter): George became “the most distinguished soldier of Connecticut in her early settlement, except Maj. John Mason.”

### Jointure — £300 from Ann’s brother John (Hartford, 3 May 1662)

> There is no date of the marriage of Capt. George Denison and Ann Borodell, but he was doubtless married in England. Pending their courtship an agreement was made between them, which was afterwards ratified and confirmed at Hartford, Conn., May 3, 1662, as follows: “This witnesseth that I, George Denison, of Southertown, in Connecticut jurisdiction in New England, for and in consideration of a jointure due unto my now wife, Ann Borodell Denison, upon marriage and upon my former engagement, in consideration of the sum of three hundred pounds by me received of Mr. John Borodell, which he freely gave to my wife, his sister, Ann Borodell Denison…”

Wheeler: First Book of Connecticut State Records, Hartford, p. 274 — “proof positive of the marriage.” Children of George × Ann begin with **John B., b. 14 Jul 1646**.

**Note:** The jointure names **Mr. John Borodell** as Ann’s **brother** (not father). Baldwin’s nurse story names a **father** John. Same given name, two men in the Borodell house — do not collapse them.

## Supports

- Supports: Local-historian retelling of Daniel’s Marston Moor / prisoner / 1645 return with Ann; England marriage inferred; **£300** settlement from **brother** John Borodell recorded 1662; John as eldest child of the couple
- Does not support: Naseby wound (Wheeler omits it); father-John as the £300 payer; Cromwell named in this excerpt (Wheeler follows Daniel)
