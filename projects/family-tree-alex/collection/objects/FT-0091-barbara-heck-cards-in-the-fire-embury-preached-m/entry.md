## Catalog note

Kid-ready **origin story of North American Methodism** — and it sits **one marriage away** from the Shorts orphans: Frances married Barbara’s son Jacob.

## Full text

### The cards and the cousin (classic founding vignette)

Griffith / Asbury tradition (public domain reprint):

> In 1765 or 1766, Mrs. Heck visited her brother… On entering the house she found several parties playing cards. It stirred her righteous soul, and, catching up the cards, she threw them into the fire and exhorted the astonished company to repent. From there she went straight to the house of her cousin, **Philip Embury**, where she… besought him to take up his cross and preach… At last he consented. The energetic Barbara then went out, found four persons whom she brought into Embury’s house and the five composed the **first Methodist congregation** in the present city of New York.

### Blue Church monument (Augusta, Ontario)

> BARBARA HECK.  
> Born 1734. Died August 17, 1804.  
> Barbara Heck put her brave soul against the rugged possibilities of the future, and, under God, brought into existence American and Canadian Methodism, and between these her memory will ever form a most hallowed link.  
> In memory of one who laid foundations others have built upon.

French notes the Methodist body cares for the Heck plot; monument erected by public subscription (~$3000, 1909), visited by pilgrims.
