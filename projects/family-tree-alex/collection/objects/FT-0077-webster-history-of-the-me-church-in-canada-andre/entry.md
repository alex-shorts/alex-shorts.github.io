## Catalog note

ME denominational history placing **Andrew Prindle** as the **first Canadian-born itinerant** entering the ranks (1806, Ottawa), then among the Canadian preachers who stayed through the War of 1812 — classic horseback-circuit context married into the Shorts line via Hannah.

## Full text

### First Canadian itinerant (Webster)

In 1806 Rev. Thomas Whitehead … and Andrew Prindle, who was the first Canadian itinerant that entered the ranks, were received on trial by the New York Conference, and appointed to their respective fields of labor, Mr. Whitehead being sent to Niagara, and Mr. Prindle to the Ottawa.

Chapter contents also summarize: “Andrew Prindle, the first Canadian itinerant, enters the work, 1806.”

### Niagara / wartime colleagues

Appointment line: “Niagara — Andrew Prindle, Ninian Holmes.”

The preachers who were engaged in the work in Canada during the war were, H. Ryan, T. Whitehead, John Reynolds, A. Prindle, E. Burdock, G. W. Densmore… David Culp… David Youmans, William Brown and Ezra Adams…

Mr. Ryan, Presiding Elder of the Upper Canada District, called a Conference of the preachers who remained in the country, which met at Mr. Benjamin Corwyn’s, near Lundy’s Lane, in the summer of 1812… There were present… H. Ryan, Thomas Whitehead, A. Prindle, John Reynolds, E. Burdock, G. W. Densmore, J. Rhodes, E. Pattie, and David Culp.

There were present at the Conference of 1814, Henry Ryan, Thomas Whitehead, E. Burdock, John Reynolds, A. Prindle, J. Rhodes, E. Pattie, T. Madden, Daniel Pickett, and David Culp.

### Augusta appointment (post-war list)

> Augusta — Thomas Madden, Andrew Prindle.
