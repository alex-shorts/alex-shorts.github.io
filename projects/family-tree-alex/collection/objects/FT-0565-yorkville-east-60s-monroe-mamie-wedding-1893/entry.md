## Yorkville when they married (~1893)

Historic Districts Council, *Six to Celebrate — Yorkville*:

> After two major economic depressions … the area was finally primed for a major wave of construction in the late 1870s with the arrival of mass transit. The **Third Avenue elevated train** was completed in 1877-78, and the **Second Avenue elevated** in 1879-80. … **east of Third Avenue became known as Yorkville**, a thriving immigrant community, which in many ways was a **second-generation immigrant neighborhood** — the place that recent arrivals aspired to live after filtering through the older, more congested downtown districts like the Lower East Side.

> Its housing stock was largely composed of **tenements**, but they were brand new and built after the **Tenement Law of 1879** ("Old Law"), so were subject to improvements. There were **abundant employment opportunities**, notably in the neighborhood's **breweries** and at the **Steinway piano factory** in Queens …

> The largest and perhaps most visible immigrant group in Yorkville was the **Germans** … they began moving uptown from … **Kleindeutschland (Little Germany)** by the early 1880s. … **East 86th Street** became the community's main artery.

On Jewish presence in the same era (Ephemeral New York, citing Harpo Marx, *Harpo Speaks…About New York*, 1961):

> … in the **1890s** … it was **"a small Jewish neighborhood squeezed in between the Irish to the north and the Germans to the South in Yorkville."**
