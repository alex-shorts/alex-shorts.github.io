## The steamer (Norway Heritage)

S/S **Allemannia (1)**, Hamburg America Line:

- Built **1865**, Southampton (C. A. Day & Co.); **2,695** gross; **315 × 41** ft
- Maiden voyage **17 Sep 1865** Hamburg–Southampton–New York
- Later re-engined (1872), sold/renamed Oxenholme (1880), wrecked Brazil (1894)

ISTG header for the Mamlock voyage matches the same named HAL steamer (Master **E. Meier**, burthen **2620** tons).

Source: [Norway Heritage — Allemannia (1)](https://norwayheritage.com/p_ship.asp?sh=alle1)

## Castle Garden in their year (NPS)

When Rosa’s party landed in **1867**, the Battery depot had already been processing immigrants for twelve years. NPS notes Germans were the **largest ethnic group** through Castle Garden overall; the depot’s **Labor Exchange** opened the same year (**1867**). Processing meant registry, baggage stalls, currency exchange, and (for many) onward rail tickets — the same facility class described for the later Solomon window on [FT-0420](../FT-0420-castle-garden-process-and-1888-rush-solomon-era/entry.md), a generation earlier.

Source: [NPS — Castle Garden Emigrant Depot](https://www.nps.gov/cacl/learn/historyculture/castle-garden-emigrant-depot.htm)
