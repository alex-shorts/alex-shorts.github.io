## Catalog note

**Gloria's father's WWI world.** Irvin Carl Gatz's burial marker at Olive Lawn reads **PVT · STU ARMY TNG CORPS · WORLD WAR I** (FAG 168568492 / person media). This Accession packages what the **Students' Army Training Corps** was on the Kansas prairie college circuit — without inventing Irvin's unit beyond the stone.

## Headstone inscription (held on person card)

From `irvin-carl-gatz` media note (FAG 168568492):

> IRVIN CARL GATZ / KANSAS / **PVT STU ARMY TNG CORPS** / **WORLD WAR I** / JULY 31 1891 — MARCH 13 1956

McPherson cenotaph shared with Martha (116548657) — body at Olive Lawn, California.

## What SATC was (open narrative)

From [KU Spencer Library — SATC Edition](https://blogs.lib.ku.edu/spencer/throwback-thursday-satc-edition/) (Oct 2018, citing Oct 1918 sources):

> On **October 1, 1918**, almost **2,500** young men were inducted into KU's unit of the **Student Army Training Corps (S.A.T.C.)**… the primary purpose… to utilize the executive and teaching personnel and the physical equipment of the **educational institutions** to assist in the training of our new armies… conducted in about **600 colleges, universities, professional, technical and trade schools**…

From [Lawrence Journal-World history piece](https://www2.ljworld.com/news/history/2018/nov/03/world-war-i-in-lawrence-ku-students-enrolled-in-mandatory-military-training/) (Nov 2018):

> In **September 1918**, Kansas inaugurated **SATC**. All male students entering the University of Kansas in September were **automatically enrolled**… barracks built… **military drill** early mornings… **eleven hours** of classwork in "war subjects"… The SATC **demobilized in December** following the **armistice**.

[Kansas GenealogyTrails SATC list](http://genealogytrails.com/kan/1918satc.html) names Kansas county quotas sent to SATC sections — useful class color; Irvin's county row not verified this pass.

## Say with kids

Grandpa Irvin's stone says he served in the **Student Army Training Corps** during the Great War — a program that turned college campuses into training camps for a few frantic months in **1918**, then stood down when the war ended. We hold the **marker words**, not his campus diary.
