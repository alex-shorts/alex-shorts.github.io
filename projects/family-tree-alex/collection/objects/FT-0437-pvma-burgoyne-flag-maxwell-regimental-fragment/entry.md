## Catalog note

Tangible Saratoga / colors beat. PVMA holds (1) a silk scrap Maxwell brought home from **Burgoyne’s surrender** colors, and (2) a fragment of **his own** regimental flag (eight-point red star on homespun). Tellable for kids without inventing battlefield dialogue.

## Full text

### American Centuries — Remnant of Burgoyne’s flag (catalog 1880.027.01)

> General John Burgoyne’s plan to end the American rebellion by launching an invasion from Canada ended in defeat at Saratoga, New York. Burgoyne surrendered his entire army of 5,000 men and his colors to General Horatio Gates on October 17, 1777. Colonel Hugh Maxwell of Charlemont, Massachusetts, was at Saratoga and took home with him a memento of his presence at this turning point of the American Revolution: a small piece of one of Burgoyne’s silk flags.

Dimensions noted on the page: Height 6.00 in; Width 2.75 in; Material Cloth.

### Revolution Happened Here — Flag Fragment (Maxwell’s own colors)

> Piece of Col Hugh Maxwell's regimental flag - 11.5" x 6.5", Charlemont, Heath

Label narrative (authored museum text):

> This eight-point red star on homespun white linen comes from a flag once owned by Colonel Hugh Maxwell of Charlemont. It’s unknown what the full flag looked like. Maxwell, an Irish immigrant with an impressive resume of military engagements (Fort William Henry during the French and Indian War, Bunker Hill, Trenton, Princeton, Saratoga, Monmouth), was remembered for embodying the sentiments of the flag. … When it came time to name the section of Charlemont that was incorporating as a new town, Maxwell pushed for naming the town Heath, in honor of his commanding officer, General William Heath.
