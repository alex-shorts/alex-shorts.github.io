## German Jewish Hartford (open authored narrative)

### Becker & Pearson, *American Jewish Archives Journal* (1979)

On origins and German immigration through New York into Hartford:

> Certainly the law [1843] made Connecticut and Hartford a more attractive place to reside for those **German Jews** who, upon entering the United States through the New York port, either chose or felt compelled to settle in communities other than Manhattan.

On occupations and the clothing trade (mid-century):

> Drawing upon their experiences in the German Dorf, German Jews offered their services to Hartford citizens as grocers, butchers, merchants, jewelers, **tailors**, tobacconists, opticians, horse dealers and boarding house owners. By **1855** Jews owned **thirteen of the twenty-seven retail clothing stores** in Hartford and **all eight of the fancy goods stores**.

On geography:

> Most Jewish businesses were located on **Main and Front Streets**, while most Jews lived on Congress, Buckingham, Governor, and Kilbourn streets, Wethersfield, Charter Oak and Retreat avenues…

On civic visibility (same decade Monroe was a child in Hartford):

> …a front page story in the September 28, **1875**, Hartford Courant tells us that **ten to twelve thousand** people attended [Beth Israel’s Charter Oak Avenue cornerstone laying], including Mayor Sprague, the lieutenant governor, several judges and ministers…

Source: [Becker & Pearson PDF (AJA)](https://sites.americanjewisharchives.org/publications/journal/PDF/1979_31_02_00_becker_pearson.pdf)

### CTHistory.org — Hoffman (2022)

> By the 1840s … the first permanent group of approximately **200** Jewish settlers established a community in Hartford. … Coming primarily from **Germany and Austria**, they settled in the downtown area of **Front, State, and Windsor Streets** near the Connecticut River docks. They found work in diverse occupations, from cutting meat to repairing watches, to trading and teaching. Some founded their own businesses.

> It was not until **1843** that groups from Hartford (Congregation Beth Israel) … successfully petitioned to have the laws changed to allow them the same religious rights as Christians.

Source: [Tradition and Transformation Define Hartford’s Jewish Community](https://connecticuthistory.org/tradition-and-transformation-define-hartfords-jewish-community/)
