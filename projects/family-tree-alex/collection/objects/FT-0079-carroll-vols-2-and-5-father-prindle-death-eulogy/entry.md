## Catalog note

Carroll’s death eulogy for **Andrew Prindle** (compeer of William Case; “Father Prindle” in late superannuation) plus the Bay of Quinte **local** roster naming **Augustus Shorts, Deacon** — documents shared ME geography/culture without inventing a training or son-in-law link.

## Full text

### “Father Prindle” as aged superannuate helper (Carroll, vol. 5)

The Rev. Ozias Barber needed an assistant at Ingersoll, but was fain to get on with an aged and unwieldy superannuate, Father Prindle, whose original thought made up for the lack of activity.

### Death eulogy — Andrew Prindle as Case’s compeer (Carroll, vol. 5, §129)

But Prindle — Andrew Prindle, — the coeval in years and the compeer in ministerial labors of Case, the man of massive proportions — of body and mind, — the acute, original theologian and mighty preacher, had just crossed the flood before his early friend, having been barely permitted to enter the climacteric year of his ministry, in the seventy-fifth year of his age (for he died January 15th, 1855), all of which might have been interpreted as premonitory of his own sudden removal.

Index/contents line: “Three young men— one middle-aged, and one aged passed away, Prindle.”

### Bay of Quinte Circuit — local preachers including Augustus Shorts (Carroll, vol. 2)

The “existing members” were reported as follow:

**BAY OF QUINTE CIRCUIT**

- Darius Dunham, Local Elder.  
- Augustus Shorts, Deacon.  
- Daniel Steel, [local]  
- George Sills, Preacher.  
- Joseph Murdock, [local]

### Rural bard “son of Augustus Short” (Carroll, vol. 2 — kinship language check)

> A rural bard, — Andrew, I think, the son of Augustus Short, the somewhat famous local preacher, — embodied the sad catastrophe in some verses…

(This “Andrew” is a **son of Augustus Short/Shorts**, not Rev Andrew Prindle; OCR/spacing varies Short vs Shorts.)

## Notes

- **Relationship clarification:** Carroll never calls Augustus Prindle’s son-in-law. Augustus is a **local Deacon** on Bay of Quinte; Andrew is a **traveling** (later Wesleyan) itinerant who also served Bay of Quinte **1813–1814** per charge list (FT-0032). Shared circuit culture ≠ mentoring claim.
- Nickname parallel only: “Father Prindle” (Carroll) ↔ “Father Shorts” (Van Deursen / Pringle compiled, FT-0035) — affectionate aged-preacher style, not evidence Andrew trained Augustus.
- Vol. 2 companion: https://archive.org/details/casehiscotempora02carruoft
- Stevenson Lundy’s Lane pamphlet (Queen’s / Scholaris) repeats the “no schools and no books” + sit-while-preaching corpulence anecdote already covered by Carroll vol. 1 / FT-0075 — not reminted.
