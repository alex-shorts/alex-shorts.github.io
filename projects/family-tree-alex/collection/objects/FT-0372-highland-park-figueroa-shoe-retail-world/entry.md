## What open local narratives say about the strip

**Charlotte Hildebrand, Jewish Journal (16 Sep 1999)** — driving north on **Figueroa** in Highland Park you pass neighborhood shops and come to **El Paso Shoe Store**, “where families from the neighborhood shop to get a good bargain on shoes.” Same corridor hosted working-class retailers from the early 1920s; **Temple Beth Israel** of Highland Park & Eagle Rock dedicated **1929** on Monte Vista — peak congregational life **1930s–50s**.

**Iver’s department store (Wikipedia, citing LA Times):** heart of the Highland Park commercial strip on **Figueroa** from **1913** through closure **1984** (5801 N. Figueroa) — the big draw that fed foot traffic to smaller shops.

**LA Times (3 Oct 1985) — “The Avenue” oral-history project:** longtime residents still call Figueroa (formerly Pasadena Avenue) **“the avenue”**; volunteer video history of ten-cent hamburgers, movie tickets, and the big red trolley — streetcar-era shopping district memory.
