## Catalog note

**Obituary** for **John A. / John Adair Blakely** (last surviving member W.T. Sherman Post No. 66 G.A.R., Platteville). Names parents **Robert × Malvina Endicott**, marriage **26 Apr 1868** to **Emma A. ## Full text

The old G.A.R. flag, a bit tattered with wear, appropriately draped with a black crepe, made its last trip to the cemetery Monday afternoon. And by strange coincidence it accompanied the body of him who generally carried it there when his comrades passed away one by one. The same flag used for each Platteville Civil War Veteran was used for its last surviving member- J.A. Blakely. One of the most active members in the G.A.R. post, Mr. Blakely is mourned by the entire community. He took a prominent part in Memorial services, even s late as last May and was always interested in community affairs. With his passing, Platteville has seen the parade of Civil War soliders pass on one by one to their final rest. John A. Blakely, son of Robert and Malvina Endicott Blakely, was born Dec. 23rd, 1847 in the Town of Harrison, Grant County, Wis., and passed peacefully away at his home in the city of Platteville on Friday evening, Sept. 10th, 1937, at the age of 89 years, 8 months and 18 days. He was a direct descendant of John Endicott of the Massachusetts Bay Colony. Left an orphan at the tender age of two and one half years, he was taken into the home of his grandfather, John A. Endicott. Here, his early boyhood was spent where he attended the district school in the town of Harrison. At the age of eleven, he began working on farms in the vicinity for board, room and clothes, continuing his studies at the school in the winter months only. On August 29th, 1864, Mr. Blakely enlisted in Company D. 43rd, Wisconsin Volunteer Infantry at Dickeyville, Wis., when he was but 16 years of age. He was on detached service with the Union forces during the battle of Nashville, Tenn., and was under heavy fire for several days. His detachment was then ordered to pursue the Southern bushwackers which was a dangerous form of guerrilla warfare. At the close of the Civil War he was honorably discharged at Milwaukee, Wis., on July 7, 1865. On April 26th, 1868, he was united in marriage with Emma A. Moore of the town of Paris. They lived on the Blakely farm for thirty years and then moved to the village of Rockville where Mr. Blakely engaged in lead and zinc mining for three years. They then moved to Platteville where they have since made their home. Six children were born to Mr. and Mrs. Blakely, one child having died in infancy, Jeanette, Mrs. John Hogan who died in the year 1915, Laura, Mrs. Ernest Jentz of Platteville, Glendora of Bloomington, Ill., George who died in the year 1918 and Lillian of Milwaukee, Wis. During his thirty years of residence in the Town of Harrison, Mr. Blakely took an active part in community affairs serving as Chairman of the Town, a member of the County Board for several terms, Town Assessor and a member of the District School board for twenty-three years. He was a charter member and the last surviving member of the famous W.T. Sherman Post No. 66, Grand Army of the Republic. He was a devoted and enthusiastic member of the Post having served in its various offices and as its Commander during the past several years. He served with a full measure of devotion his comrades to the last, calling on them in their illnesses and comforting them and finally ministering the last patriotic rites at their gravesides. On patriotic occasions, and especially on Memorial Day, he was faithful to that sacred trust of his comrades. It is significant that he read his last ritual service on Memorial Day, May 30, 1937 at the G.A.R Plot in the Greenwood Cemetery, when he was so frail that it was necessary that he be seated through the greater part of it. Mr. Blakely, despite his poor health for a number of years, remained cheerful and interested in current affairs. He always had an optimistic outlook on life, a keen sense of humor which always broke through the gloom and cheered and inspired those about him. He was an upright and useful citizen, a defender of the Republic, a devoted husband and father, a kind and sympathetic friend and a good neighbor. Surely the world is better for his having lived and served in it. Besides the aforementioned devoted wife and three daughters, his loss is mourned by six grandchildren and six great-grandchildren. Funeral services were held Monday afternoon at the Congregational Church. The Woman's Relief Corps conducted their ritual service at the church. The military honors were rendered at the graveside by the Spanish American War Veterans, The American Legion and Auxiliary and the military escort was provided by Company M. 128th, Infantry Wisconsin National Guard.

*Platteville Journal and Grant County News 15 Sep 1937*
