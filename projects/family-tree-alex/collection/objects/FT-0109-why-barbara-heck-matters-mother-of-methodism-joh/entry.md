## Catalog note

**History lesson:** Why textbooks call Barbara Heck the **“Mother of American Methodism”** (and Ontario plaques “founder of Methodism in Upper Canada”).  
**Family hinge:** Her son **Jacob** married orphan **Frances Shorts** (Blue Church stones). Generations later, **Don Shorts** (Richard’s brother) still points visitors to that yard.

## Why she matters (three beats)

### 1. She forced the first Methodist preaching in New York

Irish-Palatine Methodists in New York had drifted. Open church histories (John Street UMC museum; standard Stevens/Asbury tradition) retell: Barbara found friends at cards, threw the cards in the fire, and told cousin **Philip Embury** he must preach or answer for their souls. She gathered the first small congregation in Embury’s home (**~1766**) — the seed of what became **John Street** Methodist Church, still an active United Methodist congregation and often called the oldest continuous Methodist society in the Americas.

> Classic charge (as retold): “Philip, you must preach to us or we shall all go to hell together…”  
> — Methodist founding lore (see FT-0091 for fuller cards vignette)

### 2. She helped plant the first Methodist preaching house in America

Within two years the society outgrew parlor and loft. Tradition holds Barbara was among those who pushed to buy land on **John Street** and build Wesley Chapel (**opened 1768**). Embury (a carpenter) worked on the building; Barbara is said to have whitewashed walls. That chapel line is the ancestor of today’s John Street UMC.

### 3. She carried Methodism into Upper Canada

Loyalist upheaval moved the Hecks north. Settled in **Augusta** Township; their son Samuel led an early Upper Canadian Methodist class (**~1788**). Barbara died **17 Aug 1804** and was buried in the Augusta burying yard — the ground the Ontario plaque marks before any lasting church stood. Later monuments (cenotaph tradition; large **1909** memorial) call her the link between American and Canadian Methodism: she “laid foundations others have built upon” (FT-0091 monument text).

Ontario Heritage plaque (Blue Church):

> …nothing was built by 1804 when **Barbara Heck, the founder of Methodism in Upper Canada**, was buried here.

## Honest limits

- Cards-in-the-fire is **cherished lore** retold for centuries; treat as tradition, not a courtroom transcript.
- Fame grew as Methodism grew — historians note institutions glorify founders; still the open primary sites (John Street; Blue Church) keep her name on the landscape.
- Do not soft-ingest Barbara as a graph person this pass unless Alex asks — she remains in-law / FAN of Frances.
