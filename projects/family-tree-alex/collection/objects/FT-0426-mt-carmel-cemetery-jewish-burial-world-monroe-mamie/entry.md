## Catalog note

**Burial-world story.** Monroe Cohn (d. **25 Dec 1921**) and Mamie Mamlock Cohn (d. **4 Jun 1945**) are both recorded as buried at **Mt. Carmel**. This Accession packages what that cemetery *was* in the NYC Jewish world — founded **1906**, Cemetery Belt, Honor Row / Workmen’s Circle pantheon — without inventing which section or neighboring stones hold our people (no open Monroe FAG memorial).

## What Mt. Carmel was (authored local history)

Mary French, *New York City Cemetery Project* (2011), open narrative:

> Mount Carmel Cemetery consists of two large sections that straddle Mount Neboh Cemetery in Glendale, Queens. Old Mount Carmel was founded in **1906** on a large parcel of rolling terrain … A decade later, New Mount Carmel was established … Together the two sections contain about **100 acres** and over **85,000** interments and include the gravesites for some of the most important individuals in Jewish American history.

On the **Workmen’s Circle / Honor Row** (Eastern European immigrant working-class memorial culture):

> The Honor Row at the entrance to the Workmen’s Circle plot at Old Mount Carmel is home to a pantheon of artistic and political heroes of the Eastern European immigrant working class of late 19th-early 20th century America. Buried here are dozens of labor leaders and writers who gave voice to the Jewish proletariat, including Meyer London … Abraham Cahan … Saul Yanovsky, and … Morris Winchevsky.

On **Sholem Aleichem** and the same year as Monroe’s burial:

> Sholem Aleichem’s 1916 funeral drew hundreds of thousands of mourners and was the largest New York City had seen at that time. He was originally interred at neighboring Mount Neboh Cemetery but was **reinterred at Mount Carmel when the Workmen’s Circle created the Honor Row in 1921**.

Source: [NYC Cemetery Project — Mount Carmel Cemetery](https://nycemetery.wordpress.com/2011/04/10/mount-carmel-cemetery/)

## Official cemetery floor

Mount Carmel Cemetery Association (Glendale, NY):

- First interment **28 Dec 1906**
- **130+** acres; **135,000+** interments (Sections 1–5)
- Named after the biblical mountain; “a tribute to many lives, and to the richness of the American Jewish past”

Source: [mountcarmelcemetery.com](https://www.mountcarmelcemetery.com/)

## Why this matters for the kids

When Monroe died Christmas **1921**, his resting place was not a generic city graveyard — it was a still-young (**founded 1906**) major **Jewish** cemetery in Queens’ Cemetery Belt, already the ground that held (or that year received) some of Yiddish New York’s most famous names. Mamie joined the same cemetery world in **1945**. We still lack a plot map or stone photo for Monroe.
