## Catalog note

**Wedding-world story.** The 1893 marriage certificate names **Rev. Dr. Aaron Wise**, address **119 E 65 St**, as officiant. That places the ceremony in the orbit of **Congregation Rodeph Sholom**, then newly settled at **Lexington Avenue and 63rd Street** — a short walk from the bride’s and groom’s East 60s addresses.

## Who Aaron Wise was

*1901 Jewish Encyclopedia* (Wise, Aaron):

> American rabbi; born at Erlau, Hungary, May 2, 1844; died in New York March 30, 1896; son of Chief Rabbi Joseph Hirsch Weiss. … In 1874 Wise emigrated to the United States, and became rabbi of Congregation Beth Elohim in Brooklyn; two years later he was appointed rabbi of **Temple Rodeph Shalom** in New York, which office he held until his death. … He was one of the **founders of the Jewish Theological Seminary**… Wise founded the Rodeph Shalom Sisterhood of Personal Service…

Congregation Rodeph Sholom’s own timeline:

> **1875:** Rabbi Aaron Wise, father of Stephen Wise, appointed Rabbi; renovated synagogue rededicated with Conservative service, using choir and organ for the first time…  
> **1891:** On September 4, first service held in new synagogue at **Lexington Avenue and 63rd Street**…  
> **1896:** Sudden death of Rabbi Aaron Wise…

So when Monroe and Mamie married in **October 1893**, Wise was the long-tenured rabbi of a German-immigrant-founded congregation that had just moved uptown into their exact neighborhood — father of the later famous Rabbi **Stephen S. Wise**.

## Honest limits

- Cert proves Wise **officiated**; it does **not** prove Monroe or Mamie were CRS members.
- Ceremony place on the form is not always spelled out beyond the officiant — treat “Rodeph Sholom world” as geographic/clergy context, not a confirmed sanctuary booking.
