## Catalog note

**Landscape unpack — not a second service record.** Daniel (FT-0406) puts George at **York / Marston Moor**. Baldwin (FT-0458) puts the wound at **Naseby**. Both battles were Parliamentary victories in which **Oliver Cromwell commanded horse**. This Accession says what those fights were, so a kid-facing story can say “Parliament vs the King” without inventing a muster.

**Timeline hygiene:** Marston Moor was **2 Jul 1644**, before the **New Model Army** (raised 1645). Naseby was **14 Jun 1645**, with Cromwell as lieutenant-general of the New Model horse. Saying George fought “in Cromwell’s New Model Army at Marston Moor” is anachronistic. “Under Cromwell” at Moor can only mean **Eastern Association / Ironsides horse** in a loose contemporary sense — and even that unit is **not named for George**.

## What the battles were

### Marston Moor — 2 July 1644 (Historic UK)

> One of the largest battles ever fought on English soil took place in the evening of the 2nd July 1644… Prince Rupert of the Rhine had only just relieved the siege on the Royalist (Cavalier) stronghold of York when he took up his position on Marston Moor against a combined force of around 22,000 Parliamentarians and Scottish Covenanters.

> At 7pm the Parliamentarian (Roundhead) army launched a surprise attack and following a confused fight lasting just two hours, **Parliamentarian cavalry under Oliver Cromwell** routed Prince Rupert’s Royalist cavalry and decimated their infantry.

> The battle confirmed how a well-equipped and trained army could win the war and **established Cromwell’s reputation as a great commander**. The Royalists effectively abandoned all control in the north of England.

Source: [Historic UK — Marston Moor](https://www.historic-uk.com/HistoryMagazine/DestinationsUK/The-Battle-of-Marston-Moor/)

Battlefields Trust (same fight): Cromwell’s horse, with Scottish cavalry under David Leslie, broke Byron then Rupert on the Parliamentarian left; later that wing “swept across the moor… rolling-up the flank of the royalist infantry.” Monument text (Cromwell Association, 1939): the Parliamentary army “left to the leadership of Oliver Cromwell supported by David Leslie completed the defeat of the forces of Prince Rupert.”

### Naseby — 14 June 1645 (Historic UK + NAM)

> The battle of Naseby was fought on the foggy morning of 14th June 1645… the 14,000 strong Parliamentarian (Roundhead) **New Model Army** took on the Royalist (Cavalier) army of King Charles I comprising less than 9,000 men, in what would be the final key battle of the war.

Source: [Historic UK — Naseby](https://www.historic-uk.com/HistoryMagazine/DestinationsUK/The-Battle-of-Naseby/)

National Army Museum: Sir Thomas Fairfax commanded; **about half the force on horseback under Lieutenant-General Oliver Cromwell**. Rupert’s horse smashed the Parliamentarian left and chased the baggage; **Cromwell’s right-wing horse** then broke the Royalist left and turned on the infantry. Charles escaped; his field army did not recover.

> The New Model Army secured victory for Parliament. It was radical as a national fighting force not tied to a region or locality… the immediate predecessor of today’s British Army.

Source: [NAM — Battle of Naseby](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/battle-naseby)
