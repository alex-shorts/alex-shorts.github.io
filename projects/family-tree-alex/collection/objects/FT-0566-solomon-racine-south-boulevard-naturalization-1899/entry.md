## What "The Boulevard" was (~1899)

Landmark West!, open street history:

> Following the approval of Central Park, the Board of Commissioners revisited the design of streets on the Upper West Side, ultimately widening what was formerly the **Bloomingdale Road**. Formally redesigned in **1869 as "The Boulevard"** planners foresaw a **stately residential street** marked by double rows of elm trees viewed from 15-foot wide sidewalks and accented by 30-foot wide planted zones …

> From getting **paved in asphalt in 1890** to being dug up in **1904 for the IRT** and relandscaped …

**Timing note:** In **1899** this corridor was still the named **Boulevard** segment of the old Bloomingdale Road — a planned **residential spine** on the Upper West Side, **before** the IRT subway (1904) and **before** the heavy Jewish settlement of the UWS (roughly one-third Jewish between 79th–110th by **1930**, per compiled neighborhood histories). Solomon's **1899** address sits in the **pre-subway, pre-garment-district-peak** UWS — different from daughter Virginia's **1902 W 35th** home at the south edge of the later Garment Center (**FT-0096**).
