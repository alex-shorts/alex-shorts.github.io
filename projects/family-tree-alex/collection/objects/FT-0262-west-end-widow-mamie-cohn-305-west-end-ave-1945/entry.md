## Catalog note

**Widow-residence story.** Twenty-four years after Monroe's death, **Mamie Cohn** died **4 Jun 1945** at **Misericordia Hospital**; cert lists residence **305 West End Avenue** and status **widow** ([FT-0024](../FT-0024-nyc-death-certificate-mamie-cohn-manhattan-1945/entry.md)). West End was the Upper West Side's deliberately quiet, tree-lined residential spine — where Jewish families had been settling since leaving Lower East Side tenements.

## The corridor

From **New Women New Yorkers** (Upper West Side essay):

> Jewish immigrants from Russia, Poland and Germany also arrived during that time … while many Jewish-American families **relocated to the Upper West Side from the tenements of the Lower East Side**. But only in **1938** did the Jewish population surpass the Irish in numbers.

> West End is exclusively a **tree-lined residential locale** — a trait characteristic of the Upper West Side.

**Landmark West** on interwar West End development (514 West End Ave, 1923):

> West End Avenue was the ideal location for a residential building in the 1920s due to its **beauty, quiet and accessibility** … tree-lined streets and grass plots, **free from car lines**, public transportation, and trucking.
