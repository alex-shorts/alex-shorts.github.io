## Catalog note

**Physical Cromwell-era story that stayed in the bloodline.** In his own will George split his war kit: the **great sword and gauntlet “which I wore in the wars of England”** went to eldest son **John** (our climb); the **rapier, buff belt, cartridge box, and long carbine** used **in the Indian wars** went to son **William** (not in this graph — name in text only). That is George, late in life, saying he had fought in England *and* in New England, and handing the English arms to the son born just after the return.

## Excerpts

Dated in Baldwin’s printing as written in his own hand; mansion left to wife Ann:

> First, I give and bequeath unto my dear and loving wife, Ann Denison, my new mansion place, to wit, the house we live in, the barns and buildings, the orchards, and the whole tract of land…

> Unto my eldest son, John Denison, I have already given his portion, and secured to him by a deed or deeds… Also, I give unto him, my great sword and the gauntlet which I wore in the wars of England, and a silver spoon of ten shillings, marked G. & A.

> Also, I give unto my son William Denison, my rapier, and broad buff belt, and tin cartridge box, which I used in the Indian wars, together with my long carbine, which belt and sword I used in the same service.

Will dated **24 Jan 1693/4** in compiled abstracts (Sibert / WikiTree citing probate); George d. Hartford **23 Oct 1694** while a deputy.

## Supports

- Supports: George’s own words that he wore a **great sword and gauntlet in the wars of England**; those objects willed to **John**; separate **Indian-wars** arms to William; Ann as widow of the mansion
- Does not support: Which English battle the sword was at (Naseby vs Marston Moor); that the blade still exists; named Cromwell or a regiment; identity of will-son William as `william_denison` (that id is George’s **father**)
