## What the city history says (1940s)

Bellflower’s official city-history page (local narrative of the wartime/postwar surge):

> In the 1940s, Bellflower’s population surged from **11,000 to 44,000**. New housing was built in spite of labor and material shortages. Households doubled up and homeowners felt obliged to rent out their extra rooms. The public schools bulged, then shifted to **half-day sessions** and still bulged. Teachers were hard to find. Seven new schools were built during the 1940s…

Also notes: Defense Recreation Committee (**1941**) → Bellflower Park land purchased **1942** (later John S. Simms Park). Four more schools **1950–1954**, including construction of **Bellflower High School**, which “made it possible to form the Bellflower Unified School District in **1956**.”

**Contradiction / flag:** Obit says Gloria finished “Bellflower High” after a **1942** arrival; city history places the named high-school building among **1950–54** additions. Treat family wording as true for *where she finished school* (Bellflower community), and the municipal building chronology as a separate fact — do not invent Excelsior vs Bellflower HS attendance without a yearbook or transcript.

Earlier city history also notes children once crossed the San Gabriel on a cable to reach **Excelsior High School** before local high-school capacity grew — ambient only.
