## Historian excerpts (Burnett)

> Inspired and angered over the attack on Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941, Long Beach women rallied to do anything they could to help in the war effort…

> By 1942 women, both young and old, could be seen in shirts and slacks, wearing the identification tag of **plane plants or shipyards** where they worked.

> …as more and more men were inducted into the military it became obvious that the only way to keep industry going was to employ women.

> In the various vocational training schools during the war years, **75–85%** of the students were women… By November 1942, **6101** Long Beach women had received training in war production classes of the Long Beach public schools and were working with men in the aircraft factories in Southern California.

Burnett also notes postwar pressure for women to return to “traditional roles” when men came home — ambient social frame only; do not invent Gloria’s postwar path beyond the held marriage (**1950**) and family residences.
