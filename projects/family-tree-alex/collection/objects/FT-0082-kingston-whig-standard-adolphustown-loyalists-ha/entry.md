## Catalog note

Local-history column on Adolphustown Loyalist settlement and **Old Hay Bay Church** as the cradle for Upper Canada Methodist saddlebag preaching. Quotes itinerant **Elijah Woolsey** (Memoirs 1793–1795 via *Hay Bay Guardian* June 1993) on traveling without a horse across bays and rivers — the classic circuit-rider hardship that frames Father Shorts’s generation.

## Full text (story excerpt)

> People of deep faith, the Loyalists needed a place of worship where the saddlebag preachers of the Methodist Church could come and inspire the congregation. In 1792, two acres were cleared overlooking the Bay of Quinte and Hay Bay and a two-storey rectangular church was built with “heavy timber-frame construction and wood siding,” Canada’s Historic Places described. The interior was spare, “with a pulpit on the short wall facing the central door and at the upper level, a gallery that runs around three sides of the building.” Plain wooden benches were made for seating.
>
> All purpose for the small community’s needs, Hay Bay Church was used for general meetings and religious gatherings. To the pioneers’ delight, their church was the location of the first camp meeting in Upper Canada. (Now called Old Hay Bay Church, the building is revered as Canada’s oldest surviving Methodist church.) However, galloping throughout Upper Canada on horseback was a rough ride for the preachers.
>
> “Some part of the circuit I had to travel on foot, being unable to get my horse across the bays and rivers,” Methodist preacher Elijah Woolsey wrote in his “Memoirs, 1793–1795” (reprinted in the first issue of The Hay Bay Guardian, June 1993). “Sometimes I had to travel 15 miles a day, preach twice, and have never sat down from the rising to the setting of the sun.” When the preacher arrived, settlers provided meals and prepared sleeping quarters at one of the congregants’ homes.

Column also covers VanAlstine’s 1784 landing, the church-as-courthouse “den of thieves” joke (criminals, not lawyers), and St. Alban the Martyr UEL memorial church — ambient Adolphustown context for the Shorts orphan settlement.
