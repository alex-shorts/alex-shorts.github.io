## Catalog note

Core escapade / vocation narrative for **Father Shorts**: teenage conversion under Darius Dunham, licensed exhorter/preacher, claimed **first Methodist ordination in Upper Canada** (Bishop Enoch George, Adolphustown 1817), elder 1835, conservative ME loyalist through church schisms, last sermon + deathbed words.

## Full text

Augustus SHORTS, b.c. July 1777, d. Jan. 12 1862; m. in 1799, Hannah PRINDLE, Dau. of William and Eunice PRINDLE of Fredericksburg [sic], Ontario. In 1782 under the faithful labors of Rev. D. DUNHAM, Mr. Shorts, then 15 years of age, was converted to God and became a member of the Methodist Episcopal Church. In 1799 he obtained a license to exhort, and in 1801 one to preach. On July 1, 1817, he was ordained a deacon by Bishop GEORGE, in the old church at Adolphustown, and Elder by Bishop REYNOLDS at Trafalgar June 29, 1835; his ordination by Bishop GEORGE being the first ordination by the Methodist Church in the Province of Upper Canada. He became one of the most able preachers of the day in that denomination, and from his being one of the early settlers of that part of the country, was well acquainted with the introduction of Methodism into Canada, and with the principal man engaged in the work. He lived to see the denomination pass through many vicissitudes, but was ever a faithful adherent to his early teachings. Being very conservative, he looked with a jealous eye upon any new forms for the government of the church. Many there were who owed their spiritual birth to his faithful Exhortations and preaching, and among this circle he was affectionately known as "Father Shorts." His last sermon was preached to a congregation of young converts, in the August previous to his death; his text being Isaiah xxxv, 8,9,10. When told by the doctor that he could not live more than a few hours longer he replied: "Amen, I have made it the business of my life to be always ready for this event, and I have nothing to do but wait the appearance of my Lord." "While in Adolphustown, in the early years of his ministry, he baptized many of the VAN DUSEN children, a son of Casper VAN DUSEN being named for him."
