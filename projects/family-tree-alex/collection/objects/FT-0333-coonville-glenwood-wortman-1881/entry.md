## Catalog note

**Secondary / local-historian.** Allen Wortman and the 1881 *History of Mills County* narrate **Coonville** (1848, Dr. Libeus T. Coon) renamed **Glenwood** by act of the General Assembly **12 Jan 1853**. Soft place context for the FT-0177 meet story (“Coonville (Glenwood) was a stop… here he met Emily E. Dupray”). No open Dupray innkeeper narrative beyond SC_A one-liner.

## Full text

**Wortman, Ghost Towns ch.4 — “The Saints March In”:**

> …several stayed on, most of them moving three or four miles north to the present site of Glenwood. Among them were **Dr. Libeus T. Coons** (who gave his name to the new community, for Glenwood was first known as **Coonville**), William Britain, Silas and Ira Hillman, G.N. Clark, J. Everett and others.
>
> The name of Coonville was changed to Glenwood. The name stems from the timbered glens which abound in the bluffs along the Missouri River flood plain.

**History of Mills County, 1881** (Glenwood Township, IAGenWeb):

> In the spring of 1848 they first came to the site of the present city of Glenwood, and located **Coonville**, a village so-called from Libeus T. Coon. … The name of Glenwood was given to Coonville by an act of the general assembly approved **January 12, 1853**.
>
> J. W. Coolidge moved to Coonville … and opened a store, the first in the place… He was the first postmaster… Coonville Postmaster: Libeus T. Coons — Est. 10 Feb 1851; renamed Glenwood, 24 Feb 1853.

## Notes

- D. H. Solomon 1876 memoir (IAGenWeb) independently dates the rename act **12 Jan 1853** and describes the two-horse hack from Council Bluffs into Glenwood — landscape only; not minted separately.
