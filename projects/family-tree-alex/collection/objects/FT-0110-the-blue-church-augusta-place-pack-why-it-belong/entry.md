## Catalog note

**Place:** the little blue wooden chapel and the old burying ground on County Road 2 between Maitland and Prescott. Not a Sunday parish hub today — mainly funeral / memorial use — but it is one of the densest Shorts×Methodism **story sites** we have.

## Why this place is special (future story seed)

Hold this for a dedicated Blue Church chapter later. Reasons it earns that chapter:

1. **A burying yard before a church** — Settlers voted a church here in **1790**; nothing stood when **Barbara Heck** was buried **1804**. The ground is older than the blue walls. Story beat: Methodism’s Canadian founding mother rests in a planned “New Oswegatchie” yard that outlived the town plan.

2. **Three buildings, one name** — Anglican frame chapel **1809** (the original “Blue Church”), burned/taken down **1840**, present chapel **1845**. The color stuck as identity. Story beat: the name survived fire and rebuilding — a visual hook kids remember.

3. **Shorts stone in the same dirt as Heck** — **Frances Shorts Heck** (d. **1844**) and **Jacob** lie here with Barbara’s monument/pilgrim site. One walk ties orphan Shorts → Mother of Methodism’s son → Don Shorts still asking the plaque page in **2010**.

4. **Pilgrimage landscape** — Open sources note Methodist care of the Heck plot, a **1909** memorial, visitors who travel expressly to see Paul & Barbara’s graves (ontarioplaques comments). Story beat: this isn’t only “our” cemetery — it’s a public Methodist memory site that our family married into.

5. **Still standing / photographable** — The 1845 chapel is a real place on the St. Lawrence corridor road; CC photos exist; annual memorial services still happen. Future story can open on the building, not only on text.

**Do not invent** why the paint is blue (local tradition keeps it; reason unknown per open writeups).
