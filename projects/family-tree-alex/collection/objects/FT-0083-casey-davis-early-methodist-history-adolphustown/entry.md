## Catalog note

Contemporary local summary of Davis’s “Cradle of Upper Canadian Methodism”: first Loyalist landing, **William Losee**’s 1790 visit and petition, **Rev Darius Dunham** as first ordained Methodist preacher in Canada (1792), first Hay Bay / Parrott chapels (1792), first classes (1791), and Hay Bay’s later use as courthouse and **War of 1812 barracks**.

## Full text (story excerpts)

### Opening of the cradle

> It was on the 16th of June, 1784, that the first landing of pioneer U.E. Loyalists took place at Adolphustown… They resided in their new homes for over five years before the Gospel was preached to them, or the Missionary face was seen. In 1790, William Losee, a young Methodist preacher, then on trial in one of the New England States, made a visit to his relatives on the Hay Bay, and preached at Vandusen’s tavern in Adolphustown and in the houses of a number of the residents, here and there. He returned to his conference at Albany the next summer, and the people sent by him a generally signed petition to the Methodist Conference, asking that a missionary be sent to them. This petition was favorably acted upon and Rev. Darius Dunham and William Losee were sent.

### First churches & Dunham

> While Losee was at his preliminary work subscription lists were prepared for two “chapels,” which were the first Methodist churches erected in the Province of Upper Canada… The first of these was located on “the northwest corner of Paul Huff’s lot of land, No. 18, third concession of Fourth Town. That is where the remains of the old Methodist church in Adolphustown now stand.”
>
> Rev. Darius Dunham was the first ordained Methodist preacher in Canada, came in 1792… An open boat had to be rowed or polled along up the Mohawk river, then through the chain of lakes and the river leading to Oswego, and then around the lower end of Lake Ontario to Kingston and up the bay. Mr. Dunham always remained in Canada… He found the hardships of an early itinerate Methodist preacher’s life too great, and retired to a farm in Fredericksburgh… He continued to preach, more or less to the end of his days.

### Subscription list (includes Arra Ferguson)

> The subscription list of the old Adolphustown church still remains… They were: Paul Huff, Peter Frederick, Elizabeth Roblin, William Casey, Daniel Steel, Joseph Ellison, William Green, William Ruttan, Solomon Huff, Stophel German, John Green, Peter Ruttan, Joseph Clapp, John Berringer, Conrad Vandusen, Henry Hover, Casper Vandusen, **Arra Ferguson**, Daniel Dafoe, Andrew Embury, Henry Davis, William Ketcheson.

### Barracks (1813)

> Then, in 1813, the old church was pressed into another public service… a lot of soldiers and volunteers were quartered along the bay… During the winter houses for shelter were scarce and both it and the Quaker meeting house… were pressed into service for soldier’s barracks.
