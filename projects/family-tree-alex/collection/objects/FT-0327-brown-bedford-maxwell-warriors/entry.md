## Catalog note

**Bedford historian** Brown: Maxwell family “brave military men” in FIW and Revolution; **Hugh Maxwell** private → five campaigns → ensign commission from Gov. Pownall **31 Mar 1759**; brother **Thompson** with **Rogers’ Rangers** (St. Francis etc.); Hugh the “**Christian Patriot**” as Bedford liberty organizer; Bunker Hill credit for Hugh and Thompson (to other towns); Old Burying Ground — **Hugh Maxwell, March 19, 1759, aged 59** (fall from horse; from Belfast/Ireland). Estate note: famous warriors **Hugh and Thompson** born on Maxwell/Butterfield farm.

## Full text

> The Maxwell family furnished some brave military men during the French and Indian Wars, and also in the struggle for freedom from British oppression. Hugh Maxwell entered the service as a private, served five campaigns and held a commission from Governor Pownall as ensign, dated March 31,1759. Thompson, brother to Hugh, was with "Rogers' Rangers" at the destruction of St. Francis and all through the French and Indian Wars. He entered the service at the age of sixteen years.

> Hugh Maxwell, the "Christian Patriot," came to the front with somewhat of the heroism and organizing power which inspired his father to lead his entire family across the ocean to escape oppression.

> Thompson Maxwell was one of Rogers' Rangers. … Hugh Maxwell, credited to Lexington (the home of his wife). … abatements… in recognition of services rendered in the troubles with the French: Hugh Maxwell…

> Hugh Maxwell and Thompson Maxwell were in the battle of Bunker Hill (credited to other towns).

> Hugh Maxwell, March 19, 1759, aged 59 years. … [He came from Belfast, Ire.; was killed by a fall from his horse.]

> John Butterfield's farm includes that upon which Hugh Maxwell settled about the time of the incorporation. The famous warriors, Hugh and Thompson Maxwell were born on this estate.
