## Catalog note

**Place world for Gloria's Kansas girlhood.** Martha Tuxhorn Gatz and daughter Gloria grew up on a McPherson County farm without electricity or running water ([FT-0005](../FT-0005-conejo-mountain-funeral-home-gloria-martha-short/entry.md)). This Accession unpacks the **open histories** of the Inman neighborhood they lived in — not inventing their specific church pew or farm name.

## Inman — railroad town among Mennonite communities

From [GAMEO — Inman (Kansas)](https://gameo.org/index.php?title=Inman_(Kansas,%20USA)) (Mennonite Encyclopedia, 1958):

> Inman, Kansas… was founded in **1887**, soon after the **Rock Island railway** was built, and was called Aiken. This was changed to Inman the following year. Approximately **1,500 Mennonites** lived near and in Inman in the 1950s… There were **six Mennonite churches** in the community.

From [GAMEO — McPherson County](https://gameo.org/index.php?title=McPherson_County_%28Kansas,%20USA%29):

> In **September 1873**, the first **Mennonite settlement** was established in the county… General Conference Mennonite congregations… included **Hoffnungsau southeast of Inman**, and **Bethel south of Inman**.

## County settlement waves (Appendix F)

McPherson County history ([Document Center View/318](https://www.mcphersoncountyks.us/DocumentCenter/View/318)):

> Two ethnic groups have had a major impact on the settlement of McPherson County — **Swedish Lutheran** settlers and those of the ethnic **Dutch, German, and Swiss Mennonite** faith who arrived by way of **Armenia and Georgia** [Russian-held Ukraine]… In about **1873**… various groups of **Mennonite settlers migrated** from the southern regions of Russian-held territory in the Ukraine and settled in central Kansas… They brought with them **hard winter wheat** varieties…

Also: **Santa Fe Trail** traces through the county; **1887** Rock Island rail line with stops including **Inman** and **Groveland** (Legends of Kansas / Cutler lineage).
