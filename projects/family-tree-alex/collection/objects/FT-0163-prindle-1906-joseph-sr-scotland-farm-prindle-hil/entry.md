## Catalog note

Colonial **West Haven place narrative** for Joel Pringle Sr's father: farm nicknamed **"Scotland"** / **Prindle Hill**; believed Episcopal Church founder with Rev. Samuel Johnson; grave slab titles him **Serjeant**; estate **808 pounds**.

## Full text

> Joseph² Prindle … was born in New Haven, Conn., June 11, 1663. He married August 19, 1686, Mary Brown …
>
> On December 10, 1691, he bought of Peter Mallary, Sr., six acres … where is now the residence of Mr. Edw. G. Mansfield, and there built a house. He describes himself as a house carpenter, as distinguished from a ship carpenter.
>
> … bought of Jonathan Atwater, for 24 pounds, a tract of 88 acres … "upon ye hill commonly called the half way hill as they goe to Milford." A part of this land is believed to have been the nucleus of the homestead, which remained in the Prindle family for 173 years.
>
> The hill naturally took the name of "Prindle Hill," and the farm came to be called "Scotland," at just what time is now uncertain, but it was so named and known for at least the last 75 years of the Prindle occupancy. He is believed to have been, with Rev. Samuel Johnson, one of the founders of the Episcopal Church in West Haven.
>
> He died March 18, 1737-8 … A small red sandstone slab marks his grave, the inscription now being nearly illegible, whereon he is entitled "Serjeant."
>
> … His estate was valued at 808 pounds.
