## Catalog note

**Dutchess County place lore** for Charlotte Bowerman Aylesworth's adoptive grandfather line: rocky **Verbank** farm near Poughkeepsie Friends; swimming hole still called **"Bowerman's Hole"**; compiler argues rocky soil (not persecution) drove next generation to Canada; **Deborah** (Ichabod's daughter) **never came to Canada** — descendants held Dutchess homestead **1902**, refuting "political persecution" migration myth.

## Full text

> Leaving Mass. shortly after his marriage with Jane Richmond in 1768, the family moved westerly along the Sound into new York State upon the border of the Hudson River. At Poughkeepsie was already a settlement of Friends; hence they sought the neighbourhood of their own sect settling in a rugged frontier section. The homestead lay near the present village of "Verbank"; and is a rocky and hilly farm. Long stretches of stone walls attest the stubborn nature of the holding; and the unremitting toil necessary to make it habitable and remunerative.
>
> This fact alone offers ample reason for the emigration of the next generation in 1790 – 96; with little justification for the untenable assumption of political or religious persecution, the favourite affectation of later generations.
>
> … the name still lingers … to this favourite pond, which even now rejoices in the unromantic cognomen of **"Bowerman's Hole"**.

> Phebe and Deborah, of the second family, never came to Canada; and it is not an insignificant fact, in refutation of the untenable claim of political persecution, that **Deborah's descendants at the time of writing (1902) are still in possession of the Dutchess Co. homestead.**
