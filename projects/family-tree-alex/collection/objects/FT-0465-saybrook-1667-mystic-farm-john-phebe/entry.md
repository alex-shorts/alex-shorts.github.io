## Catalog note

**The sword’s heir gets the river farm.** Before **26 Nov 1667**, the parents of **John Denison** (b. Roxbury 1646, the first son after the English return) and **Phebe Lay** of Saybrook signed a settlement: George’s farm **near the mouth of Mystic River** + Robert Lay’s Saybrook house (bought of John Post) went to the couple. Witnesses: Rev. Simon Bradstreet and **“Ann Denison, Jr.”** They lived on the Mystic farm. John was known as **Capt. John Denison**; d. **1698** aged 52; Phebe **1699** aged 49. This is how the England-war household becomes the next generation — the English sword (FT-0459) and this farm sit on the same man.

## Excerpt

> 7 John Denison, (George¹) b. July 14, 1646, was married Nov. 26, 1667, to Phebe Lay, dau. of Robert and Sarah Lay of Saybrook, Ct. The marriage contract or deed of settlement, arranged between their parents, is recorded in Saybrook. By this deed of settlement, executed before the marriage, the respective parents conveyed to John Denison and Phebe Lay, the farm granted to Capt. George Denison near the mouth of Mystic river in Stonington, and the house and land in Saybrook, which Mr. Lay had formerly bought of John Post. This deed was witnessed by Rev. Simon Bradstreet, and “Ann Denison, Jr.” They settled in Stonington, on “the farm near the mouth of Mystic river.” He was known as “Capt. John Denison,” held a prominent position in Stonington, and in many ways, was a man of mark. He died in 1698, aged 52 years; his wife died in 1699, aged 49.

Baldwin’s child list for the couple includes **#181 Daniel** (our next climb).
