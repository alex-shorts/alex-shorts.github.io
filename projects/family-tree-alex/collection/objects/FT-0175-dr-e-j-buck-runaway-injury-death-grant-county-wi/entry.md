## Catalog note

Death of **Dr. Erastus J. Buck** after runaway accident southwest of Platteville; extensive **Dr. McGregor** funeral address. Names Civil War service (18th Wis.), Jefferson Medical College, Wisconsin Assembly 1860–61, Platteville practice from **1868**. Children named on FAG grid include **Guerdon C. Buck** (only son).

## Full text

**Portion of the obituary published in Grant County Witness, 26 Jun 1901:**

> Dr. E.J. Buck, of this city, died on Thursday last from injuries received from a runaway about a month ago. He and his wife were driving about four miles southwest of this city when the horse took fright and ran away, throwing them both out and injuring the Doctor so badly that he never recovered. For the first week or two he seemed to be improving, but after that he gradually grew weaker and weaker until dissolution came. Platteville never had a citizen who was more respected and revered.
>
> The funeral was held at the house on Saturday afternoon last. Rev. W.H. Short read the Scripture and offered prayer; Mr. Charlton Beck sang bass solos, and President McGregor, a warm and personal friend, who came to the city about the same time that the Doctor came here, read the obituary, which we here reproduce.

**Dr. McGregor's Address** (substantial excerpt):

> Friends — A rare life has just closed. Husband, father, friend, neighbor, citizen, physician, man; Dr. E.J. Buck was each of these separately in noteworthy degree…
>
> Born in **1828 in Heath, Mass.**, he was graduated from **Jefferson Medical College, Philadelphia**… In **1856** he took up his residence in Wisconsin… **Hon. E.J. Buck**, member of the Assembly from Marquette County… championed establishment of Normal schools in Wis.…
>
> Before the month of April had closed [1861] he had enlisted as a private in a company he himself had been instrumental in raising… appointed **First Assistant Surgeon of the Eighteenth Wis.**… commissioned **Surgeon of the 18th** with rank of major… mustered out **July 18, 1865**… honorable mention in the *Surgical History of the Rebellion*…
>
> He took up his residence in the city of Platteville in **1868**, and has practiced his profession among us continuously since that time… thirty-three years…
>
> The burial was in **Greenwood Cemetery**. Pallbearers: Messrs. H.C. Doscher, J.W. Murphy, Thos. Jenkins, Jr., H.M. Gribble, J.L. Nye, and M.P. Riddlaub.
