## What the Pioneer Register is (and is not)

McPherson Public Library:

> As part of the observation of the **centennial of the county in 1970**, the McPherson County Historical Society… invited [persons] to submit names of their pioneer ancestors, as well as historical information, for inclusion in this register of pioneers. … Under the leadership of Linn Peterson… the number of pioneers… increased to over **1,000**…

So **“Charles C Gatz — 1889 — Jackson”** means: descendants later recorded him as a pioneer of that township with an associated settlement year. It is **not** a contemporaneous 1889 homestead ledger created that year.

## What “farmer / pioneer” meant on that prairie

McPherson County histories (Cutler / KS Cyclopedia lineage) describe post–Civil War settlement: claims broken on grassland, towns and churches rising, waves of immigrants (including large **1870s Mennonite / Russian-German** colonies in the south of the county).

**Important distinction for our line:** John × Sophia are **Altmark (Prussian) Germans** tied to the Hilgenfeld Falls City path ([FT-0097](../FT-0097/entry.md)) — **not** proven Volga Mennonites. Use county German-farming lore as **ambient prairie context**, not ethnoreligious identity.

Plain profession frame: a pioneer farm household raised grain and stock on recently claimed land, lived through prairie weather and market years, and buried in the county seat cemetery when the generation ended — which matches the **McPherson Cemetery** stones we already hold for John, Sophia, Charles, and Minnie.
