## What Riverside was (official history)

Riverside Cemetery Association, open narrative:

> In the March of **1906**, the Lakewood Cemetery Association was created. Comprised of **125 acres** … The cemetery was planned in the style of **Frederick Law Olmsted's New York Central Park**, with curving roads, and winding paths.

> In **1910**, the first burial took place about 100 yards behind the office. Records show the first copy of rules and regulations for the cemetery dating **1914**. Here it clearly states **"Burial of persons not of the Jewish faith is strictly prohibited."**

On scale and suburban park use:

> By the **1930's**, nearly two thirds of the **110,000 gravesites** were already sold.

> … on some Sundays, as many as **5000 cars** were counted coming to the cemetery, and public buses brought visitors directly to the main building. Riverside had become a **suburban park** for many urban families, and a trip to visit gravesites very often was accompanied by a **picnic on the grounds**.

On **landsmanshaft** sections (visitor brochure PDF cited on cemetery site):

> When Jews immigrated to America they formed **landsmanschaften** … For many a landzmenschaft the first order of business, even before building a synagogue, was to establish a **common burial site**. Many such organizations dedicated sections of Riverside Cemetery. They put **elaborate gates** at the entrance to their sections, with the names of the officers engraved on the granite lintels.
