## Catalog note

Post-war **craft** beat: Oliver Phelps hired Col. Hugh Maxwell of Heath to run the **Pre-emption Line** (eastern boundary of the Phelps and Gorham Purchase). Trial June 1788 and official survey from **25 Jul 1788** veered west because the compass followed **magnetic north** (no ~2°40′ allowance). Geneva / Kanadesaga fell outside the purchase; headquarters moved to Canandaigua. Original notes now at Historic Geneva (Prouty-Chew). Letters name a wife but not her forename here — **Bridget Monroe** is in **FT-0320** only.

**Hard:** Sullivan’s 1779 campaign destroyed Seneca towns before this land rush; Phelps bought ~2.6 million acres at Buffalo Creek, **8 Jul 1788**, after dealing with the Lessees.

## Full text

### Robortella — why the line ran west

Mr. Phelps hired an experienced surveyor of impeccable credentials—Hugh Maxwell, a Revolutionary War veteran—to make the survey. But in Col. Maxwell's trial survey of June 1788 and his formal survey in July and August of that year, the Pre-emption Line veered to the west. … He reached the vicinity of present-day Routes 5 and 20 about four miles west of Kanadesaga … and writes that, "the general course for fifteen or sixteen miles is bearing west of North."

Why was Col. Maxwell's important survey line skewed to the west? … Some say it was the surveyor's error, that he did not compensate for the variance between due north on a meridian and the magnetic north pole … Others suggest it was fraud, that members of a competing land company infiltrated the survey team … Still others write that the colonel went to Geneva for supplies, or became ill and went home to Massachusetts, and that his assistants made the error in his absence.

…by studying the notes and letters, [Milliken] was able to substantiate that Col Maxwell himself was present for the entire running of the Pre-emption Line…

On May 12, 1788, Col. Maxwell set off from Heath … The men arrived at the settlement on June 2nd…

### Letter to his wife — Kanadasaga, 4 June 1788

> You may remember that on the twelth day of May I parted with you. … This place is situated on the north west shore of the Seneka lake which is most delightful situation. … There are many Indians here of different tribes or nations, some Oneidas, some Onondagoes, some Jenesee, some Tuscaroras, all getting drunk, both of men and women, and waiting for their sachems or chiefs to come and attend the treaty…

### Letter — 8 June 1788 (Lessees holding the chiefs at Niagara)

> My health has been very good ever since I came from home, but have been very much fateagued in getting our boats up the river… I have been through the woods some to reconnoiter the land, which I find to be very good, but I have not yet began the survey, nor shall I do so, until the treaty with the Indians is over.

### Postscripts, 11–13 July 1788

> July 11—Thompson is come. … we also hear that the treaty with the Indians is concluded… July 13—Mr. Phelps is come, the treaty is finished, and finished at Buffeloe Creek… we are surrounded by about six hundred and seventy Indian men, women, and children, that are all living upon the company, and every day when they get their rum some of them … get very drunk and are very troublesome. … I have no doubt but that in the course of a very few years there will be many worshipping assemblies of Christians where now the wild beasts howl…

The survey formally began on **July 25, 1788**… Col Maxwell does not mention an allowance for the magnetic variance. He plots the line following a compass that was pointing to the magnetic north pole, and the line veers to the west.

### Letter — 8–9 August 1788

> I came in yesterday from a tour of eighteen days in the woods… My last tour was to Pennsylvania and the next will be to lake Ontario.

> August 9, 1788 … By disappointment which happened to me this morning, I have not gone to the woods to day…

Hollowell’s later calculation: Maxwell was following a magnetic compass course. The land between the old (Maxwell) and new (Ellicott 1792) lines — **85,896 acres** — became **the Gore**. NY accepted Ellicott’s line **6 Apr 1796**.

### Historic Geneva — Marks, 20 Jul 2022 (notebook on site)

He hired Colonel Hugh Maxwell (1733 – 1799), from Heath, Massachusetts for the job. … Historic Geneva has Maxwell’s original field notes … Maxwell used a 4” x 6”, string-bound booklet…

One of Maxwell’s notes, near the Pennsylvania line, stated:

> The soil pritty good loam. Then three quarters of a mile and two tally over a pritty high mountain; the timber oak, hickory, chestnut and pine to a small brook, but enough to run a sawmill – and a very good place for one.

Compensating two degrees-40 minutes was necessary to run a line true north. His June trial survey veered west from the start. He arrived back in Geneva almost 4 miles west of the village.

On August 9, he wrote his wife, “By disappointment which happened to me this morning, I have not gone to the woods to day…” … Oliver Phelps was never happy with the Pre-Emption Line. On August 21, 1788 he wrote to his land agent “to make the most thorough and exact inquiry to find whether that place [Geneva] falls within our purchase.” A month later: “I am still dissatisfied about our east line. I am sure it cannot be right.”

## Notes

- Authored local-historian (Robortella 2004) + museum curator (Marks 2022). Primary = Maxwell notes/letters as transcribed.
- **Hard:** Sullivan 1779 destruction of Seneca towns; Buffalo Creek cession; drunken-camp color is Maxwell’s own letter — keep, don’t tidy.
- Fraud-by-Lessees remains a 19th-c allegation; notes show Maxwell present every mile. Magnetic-declination error is the technical finding.
- Wife unnamed in these letters; **Bridget Monroe** stays FT-0320. **Thompson** in the July 11 postscript is likely brother Thompson Maxwell (FAN).
- FT-0320: he took **money** instead of a township in pay.

### Supports
