The Bread · historic images

God feeds his people — from heaven’s manna to the Bread of Life who gives himself.

Bread from Heaven

Exodus 16:4–15

  1. What does God say the manna will test — and what must Israel do each morning?
  2. How is this bread “from heaven,” yet still something they gather with their hands?
God feeds his people daily from heaven

Story: Morning in the wilderness — dew lifts and fine flakes cover the ground; Israel gathers what God has given. Cast: families with baskets, tents, Moses pointing. Turn: heaven’s daily provision.

Bread of the Presence

Leviticus 24:5–9

  1. Where does this bread sit, and how often is it renewed?
  2. Who may eat it — and what does that say about nearness to God?
Holy bread kept before the Lord’s face

Story: Fresh loaves set in rows on the gold table before the Lord. Cast: priest, table, loaves, lampstand cue. Turn: continual nearness; priests eat what was set before him.

Five Loaves for Thousands

John 6:5–14

  1. What does Jesus do with the boy’s loaves before the people eat?
  2. What is left over — and what does that show about his providing?
Jesus multiplies bread and still has more

Story: A boy’s small loaves given to Jesus; crowd eats; leftovers remain. Cast: Jesus, child with basket, seated groups, leftover baskets. Turn: little becomes more than enough.

Broken Bread

Luke 22:19–20

  1. What does Jesus say the bread is, as he breaks it?
  2. How does this meal look back to Passover and forward to his death?
His body given; remembered in broken bread

Story: Upper-room Passover — Jesus gives thanks, breaks bread, gives it. Cast: Jesus, broken loaf, cup, disciples. Turn: body given; remembered whenever they break bread.

New City Catechism · Q46

Q: What is the Lord’s Supper?

A: Christ commanded all Christians to eat bread and to drink from the cup in thankful remembrance of him and his death. The Lord’s Supper is a celebration of the presence of God in our midst; bringing us into communion with God and with one another; feeding and nourishing our souls. It also anticipates the day when we will eat and drink with Christ in his Father’s kingdom.

How it fits this image: Jesus is the Bread of Life — the Supper feeds and nourishes our souls in remembrance of him.

Teacher notes · further typology

Thematic banner

John 6:35 — Bread of Life copywork; the discourse follows the feeding sign.

Richer typology (optional discussion)

  • Manna → Jesus the true bread from heaven (John 6:32–35, 49–51).
  • Showbread / bread of the Presence → continual nearness; fulfilled in communion with Christ.
  • Feeding of the 5,000 at Passover season → sign beyond full stomachs to the Bread of Life.
  • Passover / Last Supper bread → body given; Lord’s Supper as ongoing remembrance (1 Cor 11:23–26).
