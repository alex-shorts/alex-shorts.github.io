The Lamb

God provides a spotless substitute so judgment can pass over and sin can be taken away.

The Lord Will Provide

Genesis 22:1–14

  1. What does Abraham say God will provide — and what actually appears in the thicket?
  2. How is this lamb like a substitute standing in someone else’s place?
God provides a ram in Isaac’s place

Narrate: mountain, knife halted, ram caught. Stress: the son lives because another dies. Draw line → “substitute / provided.”

Passover Blood

Exodus 12:1–13

  1. What must be true of the Passover lamb, and where does its blood go?
  2. What happens when the Lord sees the blood — and what does that show about the lamb?
Blood covers; judgment passes over

Narrate: spotless lamb, blood on doorposts, death of firstborn vs. spared house. Draw line → “covered / spared.”

Silent for Us

Isaiah 53:4–7

  1. What does Isaiah say the servant carries for us?
  2. How is he “like a lamb” — and why does that matter?
He bears our guilt without protest

Read slowly. Our griefs / iniquities on him; silent like a sheep before shearers. Draw line → “bears sin / silent.”

Worthy Is the Lamb

Revelation 5:6–12

  1. How is the Lamb described — slain, yet standing. What does that tell us?
  2. Why is this Lamb praised as worthy to open the scroll?
Slain yet living; worthy to redeem

Throne room: Lamb as slain, yet standing; purchased people by blood. Tie back to John 1:29. Draw line → “takes away sin / worthy.”

New City Catechism · Q20

Q: Who is the Redeemer?

A: The only Redeemer is the Lord Jesus Christ.

How it fits this image: Jesus is the Lamb God provides — the Redeemer who bears the penalty himself.

Teacher notes · further typology

Thematic banner

Revelation 13:8 — the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world. Use in narration; John 1:29 stays the daily copywork.

Richer typology (optional discussion)

  • Isaac carrying the wood ↔ Christ carrying the cross (suggestive echo; keep lightly).
  • Passover as Israel’s founding rescue ↔ Lord’s Supper / cross as the greater Exodus.
  • “Without blemish” (Ex 12) ↔ sinless Christ (1 Pet 1:19) — good for older kids.
  • Isaiah’s silent lamb ↔ Gospel trial scenes where Jesus does not answer to save himself.
  • Rev 5: Lion and Lamb together — victory through sacrifice, not instead of it.

Culmination evening

Alternate NCC

Q25: Does Christ’s death mean all our sins can be forgiven? — Yes, because Christ’s death on the cross fully paid the penalty for our sin, so God declares us not guilty but accepted as his children.
