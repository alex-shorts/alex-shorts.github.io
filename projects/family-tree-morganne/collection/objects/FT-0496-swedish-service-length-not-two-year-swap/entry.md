## Catalog note

**Myth-check unpack.** Dan heard the army hitch was **about two years**, and that two men swapped Anderson/Rudd so one could avoid or finish service (FT-0470). Independent military history does **not** describe a two-year allotted hitch or a legal name-swap dodge. Allotted soldiers were long-service croft men; 1901 conscripts trained on the order of **months** (240 days in the 1901 act), not two years.

## What the systems actually were

### Allotted / croft soldiers (to 1901)

> In 1901 the Allotment System was abandoned. Sweden then changed to a universal conscription system / draft system ("Allmän Värnplikt")…

Högman on **enlisted** (not allotted) regiments: hired on short-term contracts and served **4–6 years** at a time — still not a two-year Anderson/Rudd swap.

Source: [Högman, Late Allotment System (2)](http://www.hhogman.se/late-allotment-system-2.htm)

Wikipedia (allotment system): croft soldiers were contracted **as long as they wanted and were fit for service**; some lived under the system long after 1901.

Source: [Wikipedia — Allotment system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allotment_system)

### 1901 universal conscription (värnplikt)

Högman: first draftees called in spring **1902**. The 1901 act: **150 days** training plus three **30-day** refresher courses, **in total 240 days**. Early years (1902–1907) used only **172 days**.

Source: [Högman, Swedish conscription](https://www.hhogman.se/research_soldiers_swe_conscription.htm)
