## Catalog note

**Journey color.** The first Swedish colonists reached **Fort Fairfield** for a welcoming luncheon before cutting north to **New Sweden**. Later waves (including **~300 in 1872**) spread through Aroostook — the same county where Nelson B. Parsons would marry Hattie Foss in **1899** and list occupation **starch manufacturer**. Pack the **route and welcome**; do not invent Percy or Kate on the 1870 wagon train.

## Colonists’ route (open authored narrative)

Maine Swedish Colony (*About the Colony*):

> On Midsummer's Eve, June 23, the colony of 22 men, 11 women and 18 children gathered … for a farewell party in the Baptist Hall. They sailed with Thomas on June 25 to Hull, then by rail to Liverpool, then sailed to Halifax, arriving on July 13. They ascended the St. John River by steamer to Fredericton, where they transferred to horse-drawn tow-boats to Tobique Landing (now Perth-Andover). While on the tow-boats an infant baby, Hilma Clase, died …
>
> The colonists spent the night in a barn in Tobique. They then traveled by wagon train to a **welcoming luncheon in Fort Fairfield**, and on to Caribou for a bountiful supper and overnight in Arnold's Hall. The wagon train continued on July 23 … arriving at noon at their new home, which Thomas named New Sweden.

New Sweden guide (*nshist.html* — second-year growth):

> **300 Swedes came in 1872**, bringing the total in the state to 1300. Next year, there were 1500 in Maine, with 600 in New Sweden.
