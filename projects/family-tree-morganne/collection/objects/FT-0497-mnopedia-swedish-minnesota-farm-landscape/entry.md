## Swedish farm Minnesota (open authored narrative)

Joy K. Lintelman, MNopedia (Minnesota Historical Society):

> Of the roughly 1.25 million Swedish men, women, and children who came to the United States between 1845 and 1930, **more settled in Minnesota than in any other state**.

> A larger group of Swedes settled in **Chisago County in 1851**, encouraging more to follow them. … Three core Swedish settlements were established in this first wave: the Chisago colony and two more colonies in **Goodhue and Carver** counties.

> Many settler-colonists in this wave took land along the St. Paul and Pacific Railroad's main line, through the central region of the state. Swedish American communities emerged in **Wright, Meeker, and Kandiyohi** counties and in towns like Cokato, Litchfield, and Willmar.

> Housing in the first years might consist of a **one- or two-room log cabin** that became more and more cramped as family size increased. Severe weather and long distances between villages or towns required careful planning to survive harsh winters.

> By **1890** nearly **100,000 Swedes** resided in Minnesota. This migrant stream was dominated by single rather than family migration and continued through **1893**.

Source: [MNopedia — Swedish Immigration to Minnesota](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/swedish-immigration-minnesota)
