## Catalog note

**Place color for kids.** George’s obituary says the family ran a market on the corner of **8th and Joy** in Corona for many years, and **the building still remains** (**FT-0008**; Dan repeated that in **FT-0470**). This Accession packages why that corner sits in a lemon-packing town named in part for founder **George L. Joy** — without inventing the store’s hours, goods, or a Sunkist paycheck.

## Personal facts (held elsewhere)

- Family to USA **1931**; market at 8th & Joy; George delivering by bicycle from age 10; later his own liquor store — **FT-0008**

## Corona / Joy Street (open authored narrative)

City of Corona Heritage Room, *History of Corona*:

> In **1886** Robert B. Taylor, Adolph Rimpau, **George L. Joy**, A.S. Garretson, and Samuel Merrill purchased 12,000 acres… The South Riverside Land & Water Company was formed to develop the newly acquired land… The company… introduced the building blocks of the **citrus industry** to their new town, South Riverside.

> By the time Corona hosted its third road race, it had already established itself as the **“Lemon Capital of the World”**.

> **1982** — **Sunkist plant at Joy & the railroad tracks** closes.

A Carnegie Library opened at **8th & Main** in 1906 — same numbered street as the family corner, different intersection.
