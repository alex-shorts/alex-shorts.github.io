## Catalog note

**Parish-country color.** **Klaus Jorgen Rud** was baptized at **Bellingham**, Lac qui Parle County, in **1893** (**FT-0508**). Neighboring farm families remembered arriving **near Bellingham** in **1882**, living in **sod houses**, hearing a minister at a **schoolhouse about once a month**, and watching the **Great Northern** finish the railroad in **1887** — the landscape Claus was born into, without claiming his family's exact farm.

## Bellingham-area pioneer narrative (1958 county school essays)

Huckle family memoir (District 103), *Lac qui Parle County pioneer stories* (Archive.org open text):

> Our great grandpa hired a man from Appleton with a team and lumber wagon to take them to grandma's uncle's farm **near Bellingham**.

> Grandpa told us that their neighbors had mostly **sod houses**. Most of these neighbors had been settlers at Watson. **There was no church, but a minister came to a schoolhouse about once a month** to hold services.

> Grandma helped twist hay to be used for fuel.

> Two railroad companies started building railroad grades west of Appleton. One went bankrupt and the **Great Northern completed its work**. That is why we can still see today the old railroad grades near Bellingham.

Schlosser memoir (same volume):

> **The railroad came through in the year 1887.** I was about 11 years at the time.
