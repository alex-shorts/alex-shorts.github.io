## Catalog note

**Place color for kids.** George’s obituary says he opened **The Encore** on **L.A. Restaurant Row** (**FT-0008**). Independent local history documents a supper club of that name on La Cienega. This Accession packages what the Row *was* — not a crime log, and not a deed proving 806 N. La Cienega was his lease.

## Personal fact (held elsewhere)

- Opened The Encore on Restaurant Row; later Jolly Fox Inn and Coachman in Corona/Riverside — **FT-0008** (no open local feature naming those Corona rooms this pass)

## Restaurant Row and The Encore (open authored narrative)

Kim Cooper, *From the George Mann Archives: La Cienega Restaurant Row* (On Bunker Hill):

> Today, our travels with George take us along that famously upscale diner’s boulevard, **La Cienega**—known for more than half a century as **Restaurant Row**. While its luster is today rather dimmed, in the mid-1950s the establishments along La Cienega were the city’s most beautiful, and their kitchens turned out meals which, if not innovative, were certainly expensive and unabashedly Continental.

> We begin a few blocks south of Santa Monica, where George recorded… the **Encore cocktail lounge and supper club**.

> In 1946, the building at **806 N. La Cienega** was home to Billy Gordon Originals… and by the late 1970s, it was the Jeffrey Horvitz art gallery. **In years between, it was the Encore**.

Source: [onbunkerhill.org/manneats3](https://www.onbunkerhill.org/manneats3/)

Jan Whitaker (*Restaurant-ing through history*): the Row came into prominence after Prohibition in the 1930s, centered at Wilshire and La Cienega; Lawry’s The Prime Rib (1938) is an early survivor.
