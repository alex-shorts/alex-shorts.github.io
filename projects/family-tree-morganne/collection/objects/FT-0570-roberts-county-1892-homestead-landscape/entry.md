## Catalog note

**1910 household county.** **Carl J Rud × Maria Rud** and children including **Claus** appear on **1910 Minnesota Township, Roberts County, South Dakota** (**FT-0506**) — the township name refers to a **Roberts County township**, not Minnesota the state. Most of Roberts County had opened to homesteaders only after the **1892** Lake Traverse Reservation surplus-land opening. This Accession packages that **local** farm-county narrative for the place the family lived by 1910.

## Roberts County homestead landscape

*History of Roberts County*, excerpted on SD GenWeb from the **1938 Farm Directory** (Dakota Directory Service):

> Roberts County is located in the extreme northeastern part of the State of South Dakota. … **Principal industries: farming, stockraising, and dairying.**

> **In 1892 the government purchased of the Indians the Sisseton and Wahpeton reservation which comprised the greater parts of Roberts county, and it was thrown open to homesteaders.** Sisseton was platted as a townsite **April 15, 1892**.

> The first actual settlement of the county began in 1869-1880 in the southeast part of the county, which was all that was open to settlement at that time.

SDPB (*In the Moment*, Carl Norquist, 12 Apr 2022):

> On April, 11 1892, President Benjamin Harrison issued a proclamation that the parts of Lake Traverse Reservation of the Sisseton Wahpeton Tribe, be opened to settlement. … Homesteaders began making claims four days later... **at noon on April 15th, 1892.**
