## Catalog note

**Profession / place unpack for kids.** On his **1899** marriage record Nelson B. Parsons is a **starch manufacturer** in the Mars Hill / Fort Fairfield world (**FT-0443**). Aroostook’s identity was potatoes — and for decades much of the crop went to **starch factories** feeding textile mills. This Accession explains that regional trade; it does **not** name Nelson’s employer or prove Ambrose or Daniel worked in starch.

## Starch industry narrative (open authored text)

The County (*A history of potatoes in Aroostook County*):

> Aroostook became a promising region in which to plant potatoes for the manufacture of **potato starch**, which was in great demand by textile manufacturers.
>
> In **1871**, the railroads came … With the railroad came an increase in the number of starch factories. The first factory had been started before the railroad came; **Alba Holmes** made over an unused woolen mill at Caribou to manufacture starch in **1871**, and in **1874**, a man by the name of **Wheeler from New Hampshire** built a factory at Presque Isle.
>
> In the early days of the starch factories, farmers contracted a certain number of acres with the factory owner, the potatoes to be bought at the factory at **25 cents per bushel**.
>
> … over **1,500,000** [bushels] was manufactured into starch in the **42 factories**, which had been erected in the County [by 1890].
>
> Starch factories were built at a number of points in the County … **Aroostook shortly became, and has remained, the nation's principal area for the production of this product.**

Maine Swedish Colony (*nshist*):

> **Starch became a major industry with several factories in New Sweden.** … Wherever there was a brook, a shingle, starch, grist, or saw mill might develop.

Charlie Fisher of **Fort Fairfield**, quoted in the same County article:

> "Here in Aroostook, it's mainly all potatoes … We build big houses and sheds to store potatoes in …"
