## Catalog note

**Name-class example.** Dan’s lore that **Rudd** was a Swedish *military* name is historically **plausible as a class**: soldier names were usually short; many were taken from the croft or rote; a farm called **Rud / Rude / Rudha** produced soldiers named **Ruda, Rudin, Rudman, Rudström**. English **Rudd** is a plausible immigrant spelling of that class. **This is not a muster for Claus’s line.**

## Example rote (open register)

Arsenalen Soldatregistret, **rote 224 Rude**, Västra Vingåker / Vingåkers kompani. Rotegård: **Rudha**.

Soldiers named at this torp include:

- Erik **RUDA**
- Erik **RUDIN**
- Anders / Johan / Olof **RUDMAN**
- Eric / Jacob **RUDSTRÖM**

Source: [soldatregister.arsenalen.se — rote 224 Rude](https://soldatregister.arsenalen.se/rote/224-rude/)

## How rote names become soldier names (historian)

Högman: soldier names were usually **monosyllabic**; the largest group was taken from the soldier croft or the rote.

Wikipedia *Soldatnamn* (citing *Nationalencyklopedin*): the name was often tied to the rote; a new recruit could take the departing soldier’s name. Same pattern as Sundby → Sund, Sundin, Sundström.

> Namnet var ofta knutet till roten och en nyrekryterad soldat kunde överta den avgångnes namn.

Source: [sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldatnamn](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldatnamn)
