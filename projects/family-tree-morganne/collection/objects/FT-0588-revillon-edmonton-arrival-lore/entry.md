## What Revillon meant in Edmonton (open authored narrative)

Phillips Lofts / 104th Street Promenade historical notes:

> They arrived in spectacular fashion, bringing their merchandise by rail on a train hauling **24 cars**, each one bearing a word or two of a sentence in huge capital letters, **"THIS ENTIRE …TRAIN FOR… REVILLON… BROS.LTD … EDMONTON…"** with **"REVILLON BROS"** banners on the last 18 cars.

> Their department store carried all manner of hardware from flooring to dishes, with a specialization in outfits for **hunters, trappers, and prospectors**. They also sold **wholesale goods to small merchants across the province**.

> Young Jean Revillon was dispatched to Edmonton … articles in the local papers about **Parisian lifestyle and culture**. A local family who rented a house to the young men was quite upset upon their departure … **empty Champagne bottles** … the **piano practically ruined**.

> … Revillon Frères gave the **Hudson’s Bay Company some steep competition**, besieged as it was on all sides by competitors such as Hislop and Nagle and the Edmonton based, J. L. Lessard.

> … the **most modern exclusively wholesale warehouse** … **automatic telephone exchange, pneumatic tubes, and spiral shipping chutes**.
