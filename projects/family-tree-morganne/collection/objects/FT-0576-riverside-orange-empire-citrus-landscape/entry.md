## Catalog note

**Place color for kids.** Morganne’s grandmother **Marion Orciere** was born **16 August 1926** in the **Riverside / Arlington** area (SSACI / family **C477**). That was the heart of the **Orange Empire** — when excursion trains ran through scented groves and Riverside had been called the wealthiest city per capita in the nation. This Accession explains that world; histories **do not name** Theodore, Rachel, Marion, or the Fife family.

## Orange Empire narrative (open authored text)

PBS SoCal (*Lost LA — When Oranges Ruled the Inland Empire*):

> Excursion trains whisked tourists through the **scented orchards** of Southern California's inland valleys. Citrus colonies from Pomona to Redlands functioned as genteel outposts of East Coast high society … **Riverside**, where Eliza Tibbets supposedly nurtured California's first navel orange trees with her dishwater, became by **1895 the wealthiest city per capita in the nation**.
>
> … the Orange Empire encompassed not just the Pomona, Riverside, and San Bernardino valleys, but also the San Gabriel Valley's foothill citrus belt and Orange County's sprawling Valencia groves.
>
> Local usage of **"Inland Empire"** … dates to at least **1907**, when the Los Angeles Herald referred to "the inland empire which embraces such cities as **Redlands, Riverside, Corona**, San Bernardino …"

Riverside County Agricultural Commissioner (*History*):

> The transformation of Riverside into a citrus powerhouse began in the **1870s**. In **1873** … **Eliza Tibbets** … planted two **navel orange** trees … By **1882**, over half a million citrus trees had been planted in California, with Riverside County home to nearly half of them.
>
> In **1907**, the University of California established the **Citrus Experiment Station** in Riverside …
