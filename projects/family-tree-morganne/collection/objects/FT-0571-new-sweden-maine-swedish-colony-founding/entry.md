## Catalog note

**Place color for kids.** Bill Parsons’s grandparents **Percy** and **Kate** were born in **Sweden** and their son Nelson’s census says the family **arrived 1872** — the second big wave into the **Maine Swedish Colony** in Aroostook County. This Accession packages the founding story Thomas and the state built; it does **not** prove which parish Percy or Kate left or that they lived in New Sweden proper.

## Founding narrative (open authored text)

Maine Memory Network (*Bringing in the Swedes*, Candace Kanes):

> Less well known, perhaps, is one notable effect of the [Homestead] act in Maine: the settlement of Swedes in Aroostook County.
>
> William Widgery Thomas, an 1860 graduate of Bowdoin College, was one of 30 consults President Abraham Lincoln appointed to promote immigration as part of the Homestead Act. Thomas spent three years in Sweden … and decided that Swedes would make the perfect immigrants to T15, R3, a sparsely inhabited part of Aroostook County.
>
> Officials in Maine and in Washington wanted the area inhabited not just for economic benefits, but to help secure the northern border with Canada …
>
> Even though not all the houses were finished when Widgery and 51 Swedes arrived, the immigrants settled in and got to work, building roads, homes and clearing land. In 1871, more immigrants arrived and by the end of the year, 553 Swedish men, women and children were in residence in Aroostook County …
>
> By 1880, the Swedish immigrants had built a community center named the Kapitoleum (Capitol), four churches, seven schools, **two starch factories**, five shingle mills, 71 miles of roads and 305 houses with 362 barns.

Maine Swedish Colony (*About the Colony*):

> When Thomas returned to Maine after the [Civil] War, he found the state losing population as many heeded the call to "Go West, Young Man!" … Thomas campaigned, with the support of Governor Joshua L. Chamberlain, to have the state recruit a colony of Swedes to establish a new agricultural settlement in the virgin forest of T15-R3.
>
> On March 23, 1870 the Legislature passed an Act authorizing a Board of Immigration and Thomas was named Commissioner of Immigration.
