## Scandinavian Lutheran county narrative

*Lac qui Parle County pioneer stories* (1958), Jackson / Engstrand memoir:

> There were no churches at first so whenever a traveling minister came into the community, **services were held in various homes**. **A Swedish service was conducted at the Jackson's home.** Those in attendance were John Engstrand, four from Jacob Dahlgren's, and the Jacksons.

> When he returned to Lac qui Parle **forty persons formed an oxen train** and accompanied him. The settlers that already were here were eager to have more people of their own nationality and religious faith come so that **as soon as possible a church might be established**.

> Soon after the arrival of the group from Carver, a church was begun … The present **Providence Town Hall is that first "Providence Lutheran Church" structure.**

Simpson settlement memoir (same volume):

> The first pastor was a **Rev. Thompson**. … When winter came they took turns having the church services in their homes.

## St. Pauli Bellingham (register context — not a rebuild of FT-0508)

Minnesota Historical Society, ELCA finding aid (M678 reel 46):

> **St. Pauli Lutheran Church of Bellingham, Perry Township, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota:** Congregational minutes, 1890–1919. Ministerial records, 1904–1949.

Genealogy Trails church inventory (1942 HRS) lists separate Bellingham congregations (St. Petri Missouri Synod from 1905; disbanded Methodist 1886–1912) — the baptism parish is the **St. Pauli / ELCA** line above.
