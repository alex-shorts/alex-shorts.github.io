## Catalog note

**Residential place color for kids.** Henderson **1931** lists **Chas Y Rudd** rooming at **10995 126th St** under householder **Ernest E. Turner** (**FT-0444**) — not a Rudd-owned home. **126th Street** sits in Edmonton's **Westmount Architectural Heritage Area**: a pre-WWI **streetcar suburb** of Craftsman and Foursquare houses that stayed largely unchanged through the 1930s. This Accession explains that neighborhood; it does not place Della, George, or daily family life at Turner's address without other records.

## Westmount / 126th Street (open authored narrative)

City of Edmonton, Westmount Architectural Heritage Area User Guide:

> During the economic boom prior to the First World War the … area developed rapidly, particularly after the **streetcar from central Edmonton was extended to serve the area in 1911**. In the decades following the First World War, the area experienced **little redevelopment** and as a result retains a distinct, era-specific historic character.

> … representative of the **streetcar suburbs** catering to middle and upper middle class Edmontonians … high concentration of well-preserved **Foursquare and Craftsman-style** homes constructed in the early 1900s.

Edmonton Historical Board — Westmount:

> A large portion of homes in this neighbourhood were built in the **land boom of 1912**. … **Westmount Heritage Area** was designated in **1997** to acknowledge and protect the unique architectural heritage along **125 Street and 126 Street** between 107 Avenue and 111 Avenue.

Source: [WAHA User Guide PDF](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/assets/PDF/WAHA_User_Guide.pdf); [Edmonton Historical Board — Westmount](https://www.edmontonhistoricalboard.com/neighbourhoods/westmount/)
